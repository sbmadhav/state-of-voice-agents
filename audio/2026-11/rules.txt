Section 5: Regulation.

Existing bank rules already cover a voice agent, and four items are new this month.

Most of the rules that bear on a bank's voice agent are already in force. October named these regimes without reviewing them, so most rows are reviewed for the first time.

Four rows carry the "new" tag, for changes since October 12. The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) changed the TCPA opt-out rules, and California's AB 1609 is confirmed as signed. Wiretap suits under California law target AI call vendors, and New York's RAISE Act puts duties on the model makers.

The summary has a US focus and is orientation only, not legal advice. Health care rules, recording consent among them, are in October, T9, with one addition in the health care notes. The author's view label marks my reading of how a rule applies.

The rules, guidance, proposals and lawsuits that bear on a bank's voice agent fall into four groups.

Disputes, collections and equal service.

Regulation E (electronic transfers). An oral notice of an error can start investigation duties, so a dispute raised to the agent likely counts. The agent should log it and hand off. Author's view. The rule is in force.

Regulation Z (card billing errors). Billing-error notices must be written, so the agent should tell the caller how to give written notice rather than treat the call as notice. Author's view. The rule is in force.

UDAAP (unfair, deceptive or abusive acts or practices) and CFPB chatbot expectations. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) names inaccurate answers, unrecognized disputes and loops with no path to a person as risks. Its issue spotlight dates from June 2023. The CFPB withdrew 67 guidance documents in May 2025 and its leadership is in litigation, but the statutes and regulations are unchanged.

Debt collection (Fair Debt Collection Practices Act, Regulation F). For collectors covered by Regulation F, more than 7 calls in 7 days is presumed harassment, and nothing about the debt may be disclosed to a wrong party who answers. First-party lenders face UDAAP and state law instead. Both are in force.

Fair lending and equal service. Speech recognition that works worse for some accents can mean unequal service or collections outcomes, so a bank should test accuracy by accent and language. Author's view. The duty is ongoing.

Accessibility. Callers with speech disabilities and callers on relay services need a route past the agent and should never be trapped in it. Author's view. The duty is ongoing.

Disclosure, opt-outs and a route to a person.

TCPA opt-out rules, FCC 26-67. Callers (here, those placing the calls) may designate one or more approved methods as the exclusive way to opt out, and must disclose them. Opt-outs can be limited to a message category, and the 10-business-day deadline is unchanged. Wireless numbers from a spouse, a call-in or another institution's records now qualify for exempt fraud alerts. Published on October 26, it takes effect on November 25, 2026.

California AB 1609 (customer service chatbots). The law applies by phone and online at businesses with more than 500 million dollars in revenue. A bot may not pose as human, a caller who asks for a person gets a good-faith connection within 15 minutes or a specific appointment within one business day, and phone holds are capped. Public prosecutors enforce it, with up to 5,000 dollars per first violation and 10,000 dollars after. Signed on September 28, 2026, it takes effect on January 1, 2027. October listed it as unverified.

California wiretap suits against AI call vendors (CIPA). Plaintiffs argue under the California Invasion of Privacy Act (CIPA) that a vendor whose AI listens to calls is an eavesdropping third party. U.S. Bank callers sued Pegasystems on October 2, 2026, alleging its voice AI analyzed their calls without consent (allegations only; up to 5,000 dollars per violation under California law). A similar suit over Google's contact-center AI survived dismissal in 2025, then was dismissed with leave to amend on October 22, 2026. A bank should therefore disclose AI and third-party processing at the start of each call, and limit vendor use of call data in the contract. The cases are pending.

FCC AI call disclosure proposal (FCC 24-84). It would require AI disclosure at consent and on the call. Proposed in August 2024, it had not been adopted as of October 2026.

Utah AI Policy Act. AI must be disclosed when a person asks, and regulated occupations disclose it up front in high-risk interactions, including financial advice and collecting sensitive data. In effect since May 7, 2025, it sunsets on July 1, 2027.

Colorado SB 26-189. The law requires notice before automated tools materially influence financial or lending decisions, and an explanation and human review after an adverse outcome. Fraud and anti-money-laundering tools are carved out. Signed on May 14, 2026, it takes effect on January 1, 2027, if rulemaking is done. October listed it as unverified.

Data security and vendor oversight.

GLBA Safeguards Rule (FTC) and bank security guidelines. The Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA) rules require a written security program, encryption and oversight of service providers, including the voice-AI vendor. Nonbank lenders report breaches of unencrypted data affecting more than 500 consumers to the Federal Trade Commission (FTC) within 30 days. Breach notice has been in force since May 13, 2024.

Incident notification (banks and credit unions). Banks notify their regulator within 36 hours of a significant computer-security incident, and service providers must tell the bank. Federally insured credit unions notify the National Credit Union Administration (NCUA) within 72 hours of a reportable cyber incident, including one at a third-party vendor. The contract should therefore cover the voice-AI vendor. Banks have been covered since 2022 and credit unions since September 1, 2023.

PCI DSS v4.0.1, requirement 3.3.1. Card security codes must not survive in recordings or transcripts after authorization, even encrypted. Speech-to-text output is in scope. The FAQ was updated in June 2025.

Third-party risk management (OCC, Fed, FDIC, NCUA). The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, the Federal Reserve, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation and the NCUA expect an institution to oversee the voice-AI vendor across the relationship. A proposal would move to a lighter, harm-based approach. The proposal dates from September 11, 2026, and comments are due on November 16, 2026.

Model risk guidance (replaces SR 11-7). It excludes generative and agentic AI by footnote, so voice agents built on large language models (LLMs) fall under general governance, while vendor models stay in scope. It is aimed mainly at banks with more than 30 billion dollars in assets (up from 1 billion dollars), and the agencies say non-compliance alone will not draw supervisory criticism. It was issued on April 17, 2026.

New York RAISE Act (frontier AI developers). The act covers the model makers, not the bank. AI developers with more than 500 million dollars in annual revenue (OpenAI and Anthropic are named) must register by January 1, 2027, and publish safety frameworks and model transparency reports. They must report critical safety incidents within 72 hours, against 15 days under California's SB 53. Fines run up to 1 million dollars per violation, then 3 million dollars. For a bank, those filings are new evidence for vendor due diligence. Author's view. Registration opened on November 10, 2026, and the act takes effect at the start of 2027.

Authentication and fraud.

FFIEC authentication guidance and NYDFS AI guidance. The Federal Financial Institutions Examination Council (FFIEC) expects institutions to risk-assess call-center authentication and layer controls. New York's financial regulator (NYDFS) advises against SMS, voice or video as authentication factors and asks for deepfakes in risk assessments. The FFIEC guidance dates from August 2021 and the NYDFS letter from October 2024. Customer multifactor authentication has been required since November 1, 2025.

FinCEN deepfake alert. The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) alert gives red flags and a suspicious activity report key term for deepfake fraud, and names voice clones in family-emergency scams. It dates from November 13, 2024, and was cited again in a September 2026 alert.

Much of this lands in two places: the vendor contract, and the agent's ability to recognize a dispute, a billing error or a request for a person when it hears one. Author's view.
