Section 1: At a glance.

Nine findings moved since October.

The first published bank results followed the pattern October expected, the reliability benchmark changed leaders, and one piece of October's advice needed correcting.

This update covers October 12 to November 14, 2026. Most of October's report stands, and nine findings moved. Two are first reviews of subjects October only named, bank results and bank regulation. One is a correction to October's advice on identity checks, and six are updates. The last column of the table says what follows from each change.

Each row sets what October said beside what this update found. Rows link to the sections that carry the sources and evidence labels.

Repeat reliability. Retell led 7 platforms, with 75.6 percent of 82 scenarios passed three times in a row. PolyAI, newly tested, now leads 10 configurations at 87.8 percent, and the 7 October results are unchanged. Leaders change monthly, so a team's own test set stays the gate.

Financial services results. October did not review them, but expected balances and payments first. The one dated result is 7 percent of all calls at an Australian credit union. Undated US case studies claim 37 percent to 90 percent, and CIBC reports more than 16 million voice-assistant calls. The expected pattern held, with routine servicing first, and all figures are self-reported.

Background speech. October found that background speech raised transcription errors to 22 percent to 31 percent. With a TV on, task completion fell from 38.7 percent to 8.6 percent in one test, and an open model now isolates the caller. This is the failure mode to test first.

Financial regulation. October named three regimes but did not review them: the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA), the card data security standard (PCI DSS) and the ban on unfair, deceptive or abusive acts or practices (UDAAP). This update maps them to a voice agent, with Regulations E and Z, debt collection rules, new model-risk guidance and the FCC opt-out order. The new model-risk guidance excludes generative AI, so voice agents fall under general governance.

California chatbot law. October reported AB 1609 as signed, but unverified. It is now confirmed: signed September 28, 2026, effective January 1, 2027, and covering phone and online customer service at businesses over 500 million dollars in revenue. Large banks need a human within 15 minutes, or a booked appointment, when a caller asks.

Identity checks. October advised voiceprint plus liveness, then a one-time code, for high-risk calls. That advice is corrected: voice is a risk signal, not an authentication factor, and deepfake detectors also miss more over real phone lines. High-risk steps move to app push or other phishing-resistant checks.

System-1 decisions. OpenAI's Decisions API was in public beta. It is now generally available, with native GPT-Live integration. Adoption becomes a contract question: data use, third-party risk and, in health care, a business associate agreement (BAA).

Open speech models. October found open models fit only for small routers. Open models now cover recorded-call transcription, speech output and voice isolation. More of the audio path can stay on a team's own infrastructure, but live transcription still needs streaming models.

Health care. October covered disclosure rules in California, Texas and Utah. California's AB 1979 adds a licensed-judgment rule and covers health care chatbots under the state's medical privacy law. Scheduling and routine messages stay exempt.
