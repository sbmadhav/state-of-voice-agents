Section 8: New releases.

What's new in the voice AI world.

The ten releases and launches since October 12 that matter most to teams building voice agents for regulated contact centers, in the author's order of relevance. This is a link list, not an endorsement; the releases that change a recommendation are analyzed in section 7. Dates are 2026.

Number 1: Scribe v3 and Scribe v3 Live, ElevenLabs, new this month, November 10. Speech-to-text models for recorded and live audio. The live model adds speaker labels and makes 31 percent fewer word errors than Scribe v2 Realtime across 102 languages. Both are HIPAA-eligible under a business associate agreement, with a zero-retention mode and EU data residency, so they can be assessed for regulated calls now. Vendor-reported.

Number 2: Retell LLM (Tell-1), Retell AI, new this month, November 13. An in-house language model that starts its reply at once and reasons silently while the first sentence plays; available from November 19 at 3.5 cents a minute. Its 94.4 average is on Retell's own vCX benchmark, so test it on your own scripts before relying on the ranking. Vendor-reported.

Number 3: Managed Agents, Cartesia, new this month, October 29. A hosted voice agent platform with telephony, knowledge bases and tool calls, at a launch price of 3 cents a minute. Cartesia reports 87 percent accuracy on ServiceNow's EVA-Bench enterprise scenarios. The announcement does not cover HIPAA or PCI terms; ask before a pilot. Vendor-reported.

Number 4: STT v6, Soniox, new this month, November 3. Speech recognition that receives the agent's own text and keeps the agent's voice out of the transcript, including while the caller interrupts. It targets agents that hear themselves on speakerphones and in cars. Soniox published no benchmark figures. Vendor-reported.

Number 5: Tofu-1, Bland, new this month, November 13. A full-duplex speech-to-speech model in research preview for a limited number of enterprises, aimed at noisy, multi-speaker calls in banking, health care, insurance and government. Bland describes a television test like the one in section 3, but publishes no measured results, pricing or compliance terms. Vendor-reported.

Number 6: Prism, Rime, new this month, October 28. A speech-to-speech model that writes each reply as text before speaking it, so guardrails can check or rewrite the words first. Rime reports 0.57 seconds to first audio and 56.6 on the tau-Voice customer-service benchmark, which Rime ran itself on Artificial Analysis's test harness. Vendor-reported.

Number 7: Pipecat 1.16, open-source framework, new this month, November 12. Adds speaker labels to transcripts from every speech-to-text service that supports them, pause and resume for Genesys AudioHook audio streams, a jambonz telephony connector and support for Soniox's agent-voice filtering. Relevant to teams that run their own call pipeline to keep control of data.

Number 8: Voxtral 3, Mistral AI, new this month, October 20. Speech-to-text and text-to-speech models with a voice agent SDK, offered on an EU-hosted API or for deployment on your own servers, which helps where audio must stay in a region or on premises. Mistral reports live transcription in under 500 milliseconds in more than 35 languages. Vendor-reported.

Number 9: Grok Voice 3.0, xAI, new this month, October 14. A speech-to-speech model in xAI's API at 8 cents a minute. The company cites a score of 86.9 on the Artificial Analysis Speech-to-Speech Index and says Starlink call centers resolve about 70 percent of 20,000 daily calls without a person. The announcement gives no compliance terms. Vendor-reported.

Number 10: Audio Decision API, Hume AI, new this month, November 2. Answers yes or no, multiple-choice and rating questions directly from call audio, such as whether a caller sounds frustrated or a voice is AI-generated, and returns probabilities. Hume reports 70.7 percent agreement with a human panel. Access is by waitlist; treat it as a signal to test, not a control. Vendor-reported.

Each link opens the maker's own announcement in a new tab. Figures are the makers' claims and were not tested for this report.
