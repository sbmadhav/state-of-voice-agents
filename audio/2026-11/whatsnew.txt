Section 8: New releases.

Ten releases to know about, in order of relevance.

These are the ten releases and launches since October 12 that matter most to teams building voice agents for regulated contact centers. This is a link list, not an endorsement.

The releases that change a recommendation are analyzed in section 7. The ten here are ranked in my order of relevance. Several bear on the problem in section 3, telling the caller's voice from any other: speaker labels in Scribe v3 Live and Pipecat 1.16, Soniox's filter for the agent's own voice, and Bland's model for noisy, multi-speaker calls. Each entry says what the release is and why it matters, and says so where a maker has published no tests or compliance terms. Dates are 2026.

The table ranks ten releases since October 12 by their relevance to regulated contact centers. The ranking is my judgment, and the figures are the makers' claims, labeled vendor-reported.

Number 1 is Scribe v3 and Scribe v3 Live from ElevenLabs, on November 10. The two are speech-to-text models for recorded and live audio. ElevenLabs says the live model adds speaker labels and makes 31 percent fewer word errors than Scribe v2 Realtime across 102 languages. Both are HIPAA-eligible under a business associate agreement, with a zero-retention mode and EU data residency, so they can be assessed for regulated calls now. Vendor-reported.

Number 2 is Retell LLM (Tell-1) from Retell AI, on November 13. Tell-1 is an in-house language model that starts its reply at once and reasons silently while the first sentence plays. It is available from November 19 at 3.5 cents a minute. Its 94.4 average is on Retell's own vCX benchmark, so a team should test it on its own scripts before relying on the ranking. Vendor-reported.

Number 3 is Managed Agents from Cartesia, on October 29. Managed Agents is a hosted voice agent platform with telephony, knowledge bases and tool calls, at a launch price of 3 cents a minute. Cartesia reports 87 percent accuracy on ServiceNow's EVA-Bench enterprise scenarios. The announcement does not cover HIPAA or PCI terms, so a buyer should ask before a pilot. Vendor-reported.

Number 4 is STT v6 from Soniox, on November 3. STT v6 is speech recognition that receives the agent's own text and keeps the agent's voice out of the transcript, including while the caller interrupts. It targets agents that hear themselves on speakerphones and in cars. Soniox published no benchmark figures. Vendor-reported.

Number 5 is Tofu-1 from Bland, on November 13. Tofu-1 is a full-duplex speech-to-speech model in research preview for a limited number of enterprises, aimed at noisy, multi-speaker calls in banking, health care, insurance and government. Bland describes a television test like the one in section 3, but publishes no measured results, pricing or compliance terms. Vendor-reported.

Number 6 is Prism from Rime, on October 28. Prism is a speech-to-speech model that writes each reply as text before speaking it, so guardrails can check or rewrite the words first. Rime reports 0.57 seconds to first audio and 56.6 on the tau-Voice customer-service benchmark, which Rime ran itself on Artificial Analysis's test harness. Vendor-reported.

Number 7 is Pipecat 1.16, the open-source framework, on November 12. The release adds speaker labels to transcripts from every speech-to-text service that supports them, pause and resume for Genesys AudioHook audio streams, a jambonz telephony connector and support for Soniox's agent-voice filtering. It is relevant to teams that run their own call pipeline to keep control of data.

Number 8 is Voxtral 3 from Mistral AI, on October 20. Voxtral 3 comprises speech-to-text and text-to-speech models with a voice agent SDK, offered on an EU-hosted API or for deployment on a team's own servers, which helps where audio must stay in a region or on premises. Mistral reports live transcription in under 500 milliseconds in more than 35 languages. Vendor-reported.

Number 9 is Grok Voice 3.0 from xAI, on October 14. Grok Voice 3.0 is a speech-to-speech model in xAI's API at 8 cents a minute. The company cites a score of 86.9 on the Artificial Analysis Speech-to-Speech Index and says Starlink call centers resolve about 70 percent of 20,000 daily calls without a person. The announcement gives no compliance terms. Vendor-reported.

Number 10 is Audio Decision API from Hume AI, on November 2. The API answers yes or no, multiple-choice and rating questions directly from call audio, such as whether a caller sounds frustrated or a voice is AI-generated, and returns probabilities. Hume reports 70.7 percent agreement with a human panel. Access is by waitlist, and the output is a signal to test, not a control. Vendor-reported.

None of the figures above was tested for this report, so the list is a place to start reading and no more. Each link opens the maker's own announcement in a new tab. The health care notes follow.
