Section 2: Published results.

The published wins are administrative, and all are self-reported.

The strongest published results so far come from health systems, and every one covers access and service, not advice.

Three of those results follow, from Tampa General, WellSpan and Intermountain. All the figures are reported by the organization or its vendor, with no control group, so some regression should be expected at scale. Where a system published before and after values, its card shows both.

The three systems report shorter waits, high call volumes and less abandonment from voice agents on administrative calls. Every figure is self-reported.

Tampa General, which uses Hyro, reports that its average wait fell from 6.2 minutes to 2.4 within two weeks.

It also reports abandonment down from 34 percent to 14.9 percent and 21 percent more appointments scheduled.

WellSpan reports that an AI agent from Hippocratic AI handles more than 160,000 patient calls a month.

It also reports about 7,000 hours of conversation, and the two expanded their partnership on July 30, 2026.

Intermountain, which uses Hyro with Agentforce Health, reports that it has automated 44 percent of its repetitive calls.

It also reports call abandonment down 85 percent on in-scope lines such as patient portal support.

Measurement trails deployment more widely, too. In a Talkdesk and NewtonX report, 98 percent of customer experience, IT and operations leaders say they have deployed AI in the customer journey, but only 5 percent can quantify its impact on business outcomes. Vendor-reported.

For financial services, I expect the same pattern as in health care. Balance, card status, payment dates and dispute intake will come first, while advice, fraud adjudication and hardship conversations stay with people.

Enterprise agent platforms are moving into health care too. Sierra says it serves most Fortune 50 health care companies, and it announced new voice models at Sierra Summit 2026, but no health care outcome figures have been published yet.
