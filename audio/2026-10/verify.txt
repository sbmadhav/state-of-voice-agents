T10. Claim check.

Of 21 circulating claims, six check out.

I traced 21 circulating claims to their sources: benchmark figures, vendor claims and one widely shared decision-offload cost model. Fewer than a third hold up as stated.

Each claim carries one of three verdicts. It is verified when the source matches it. It is partial or vendor when the source confirms only part of it or is the vendor's own statement. It is unverified or wrong when I could not find it or the source says otherwise.

The nine claims in the last group include ones about latency, about cost and about models that supposedly cannot hallucinate.

Six claims are verified, six are partial or rest on a vendor's word, and nine are unverified or wrong. Each row gives the claim as it circulates, then my verdict and what the source I checked shows.

Cekura Bench ran 7 configurations through 82 scenarios with 3 repeats, 246 calls each. I rate that claim partial or vendor. The design is confirmed, but the live hub now lists 8 platforms.

Pass three is the primary metric. I rate that claim verified. It is the share of scenarios in which all three retained calls pass.

The figures quoted for Retell, ElevenLabs, Vapi and Gemini Live are right. I rate that claim verified. They match exactly on all four reported metrics.

LiveKit scored 70.73 percent and Pipecat 63.41 percent on pass three. I rate that claim verified. Both pass three scores are confirmed; I did not see the other metrics.

GPT Realtime scored 64.63 percent on pass three. I rate that claim unverified or wrong. I could not find this figure; the separate speech-to-speech benchmark shows 79.3 percent.

The model stack listed for each platform is accurate. I rate that claim unverified or wrong. Providers chose their own stacks, and the lists are not independently confirmed.

Entity drift happens between the transcript and the tool call. I rate that claim verified. An instance with a phone number is documented.

TypeSafe Jev exists and was built by an ex-OpenAI researcher. I rate that claim verified. It was released on September 15, 2026.

Jev answers in 70 to 150 milliseconds. I rate that claim unverified or wrong. The vendor quotes 70 to 500 milliseconds; one test measured a median of 175 milliseconds and a 95th percentile of 270 milliseconds.

Jev costs 4.2 cents per million input tokens, with free output. I rate that claim partial or vendor. This matches the vendor's early-access pricing.

Jev is calibrated and cannot hallucinate. I rate that claim partial or vendor. Its output is always schema-valid, but calibration was weaker than claimed in independent tests.

Decision models are 400 times cheaper than LLMs. I rate that claim unverified or wrong. The figure is not in primary sources; the real saving in one cascade test was about 72 percent.

OpenAI Decisions API is generally available. I rate that claim unverified or wrong. It has been in public beta since October 6, 2026.

Open-source Laya is a drop-in router. I rate that claim unverified or wrong. Its routed base checkpoint scores 42.5 percent on Banking77, so fine-tuning is required.

Open models hit 78.4 percent of AI Gateway token volume. I rate that claim verified. The post says exactly that; it was a one-day record on a developer-heavy gateway.

Regulated enterprises are moving inference off public APIs. I rate that claim partial or vendor. Private cloud endpoints are common, but I found no named US on-premises voice deployment.

A decision-offload simulator shows turns of about 730 milliseconds, against 1,900 milliseconds. I rate that claim unverified or wrong. The figures are simulated, not measured.

The decision-offload simulator shows 11,017 dollars a month saved. I rate that claim partial or vendor. The arithmetic is plausible, but the saving is about 6 percent of run rate and no infrastructure is modeled.

Sierra serves 80 percent of Fortune 50 health care companies. I rate that claim partial or vendor. Sierra made the claim at its Summit 2026, and it is not independently verified.

Limited-memory models cannot hallucinate. I rate that claim unverified or wrong. The authors say the method does not guarantee perfect factuality.

Fact storage is about 99 percent of model size. I rate that claim unverified or wrong. No study measures it; retrieval models match models 18 times to 50 times larger on knowledge tasks.

The nine unverified or wrong claims should not be repeated as fact, and the six partial ones need their caveat attached. T11 turns from claims to practice.
