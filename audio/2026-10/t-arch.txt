T1. Architecture.

Three generations, converging on "talk fast, think slow".

The newest voice systems pair a fast conversational layer with a slower reasoning one. The older cascade still offers the most control.

Voice agents have been built in three ways. A cascade chains three models: speech-to-text (STT) transcribes the caller, a large language model (LLM) writes the reply and text-to-speech (TTS) speaks it. Native speech-to-speech replaces the chain with one model that takes audio in and gives audio out. Continuous full-duplex goes a step further, with a model that listens and speaks at the same time.

The newest systems share one design: talk fast, think slow. OpenAI's GPT-Live removes the separate turn detector and delegates search and reasoning to a frontier model behind an asynchronous boundary, so a slow tool call cannot stall audio. Sierra's Tandem Voice pairs a real-time conversation layer with a reasoning layer, and Gemini 3.8 Live calls tools in the background. A System-1 router, a small model that returns typed decisions and never writes prose, fits beside any of these as the dispatch layer.

A cascade gives the most control and text at every step, while full-duplex handles turns itself and offers the least control today. The table is my summary of the announcements and documentation cited, not a set of measurements.

Examples. Pipecat, LiveKit and Retell are cascades, and so is Agentforce Voice. The Gemini Live API and gpt-realtime-2.1 are speech-to-speech models. OpenAI GPT-Live, Gemini 3.8 Live and Sierra Tandem Voice are full-duplex systems.

Turn-taking. A cascade needs voice activity detection (VAD), which tells speech from silence, and a turn detector. A speech-to-speech model still relies on a turn detector to decide when to start. A full-duplex model controls the turns itself and can backchannel, giving short acknowledgments such as "mm-hmm".

Tone and prosody. A cascade loses them in transcription. Speech-to-speech keeps them. Full-duplex keeps them.

Tools and reasoning. A cascade runs them inside the LLM step, which blocks the reply. Speech-to-speech runs them in the model or blocks on them. Full-duplex delegates them asynchronously to a frontier model.

Audit trail. A cascade produces native text at every step. Speech-to-speech makes transcripts available. Full-duplex derives its transcripts after the fact.

Control and vendor choice. A cascade offers the highest level of both. Speech-to-speech offers a medium level. Full-duplex offers the lowest today, because each system comes from a single vendor.

Fit for regulated work. A cascade allows a per-step audit and per-component contracts. Speech-to-speech fits simpler flows with one covered vendor. Full-duplex is one to watch closely: the GPT-Live API is announced as upcoming.

Production adds two constraints of its own. Calls arrive over telephone (SIP) trunks as compressed 8 kHz audio, with jitter and packet loss, so tests should run on that audio and not on clean recordings. Integrations with scheduling, customer (CRM) and health record (EHR) systems are usually the slowest and most brittle part of a launch, so they should be budgeted first. Author's view.

In this reference flow for a cascade, the System-1 router decides before the language model speaks. The flow is my own design, not a description of a product.

The audio comes in from the caller's phone at 8 kilohertz and passes through echo cancellation. The caller's voice is isolated and the speakers are separated, which is called diarization. A speaker-aware voice activity detector and a turn model find the end of the turn, and streaming speech-to-text transcribes it.

The thinker, a System-1 router, returns the intent and any risk flags as typed probabilities, then picks the sub-agent and its MCP servers. Fraud, a clinical concern, a complaint or low confidence escalates the call.

The responder is the sub-agent's language model, with narrowed tools. It takes its facts from the governed store or the system of record, reads values back before any write, and hands the reply to text-to-speech.

Throughout, one correlation ID runs across the voice-activity, speech-to-text, router, language-model, tool and text-to-speech events, which makes the trace replayable.

The cascade pays for its control in time, because each stage in that flow adds delay. T2 adds the delays up.
