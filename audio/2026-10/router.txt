Section 5: Think silently, then respond.

A thinker-responder model for conversational agents.

People don't say every thought out loud before they answer. We size up the question, decide what it's about, recall what we need, and then speak. A voice agent should work the same way: a fast System-1 model is the thinker, making typed decisions silently, and a conversational LLM is the responder, turning those decisions into a natural reply.

One caller turn, two ways.

Caller: "I got charged twice for my visit last month.".

What the thinker decides (System 1, silent, typed).

Intent: which sub-agent owns this turn (billing, scheduling, complaint).

Risk: fraud, clinical, vulnerable-caller or complaint flags that force a handoff.

Tools: which MCP servers to expose, and which facts to fetch before speaking.

Turn state: is the caller done, correcting themselves, or just saying "mm-hmm".

Speaker: is this the caller, or someone else in the room.

Emotion: frustration or confusion that should change the tone, slow things down or hand off.

Confidence: a probability on each, so low-confidence turns hand off instead of guessing.

Why it works.

Faster: decisions take tens to hundreds of milliseconds, so the responder starts with data already fetched.

Cheaper: the expensive model only writes the reply, not the routing.

Safer: the "thought" is a structured record, not prose, so it can be logged, audited and gated by rules. The responder can't narrate a tool result it never received.

A deliberate difference: OpenAI and Sierra put the fast model on the talking side and a slower reasoner behind it. This design puts the fast model on the deciding side. The two combine: a fast talker, a fast silent decider, and a slower reasoner when needed.

Works with any voice stack: the thinker does not need a speech-to-text pipeline. Beside a speech-to-speech or full-duplex model it reads the transcript stream and decides which tools and policies apply, so the voice model keeps its natural turn-taking. Because a full-duplex model starts speaking within a few hundred milliseconds, the thinker also needs a stop control: when it flags risk mid-turn, it cuts the audio output at once, before protected data or advice reaches the caller.

Limit: the thinker handles bounded choices. Open-ended reasoning still needs a deeper planner the responder can call.
