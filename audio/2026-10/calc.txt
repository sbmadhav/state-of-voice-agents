Section 4: Economics.

Resolution rate drives the savings; token price does not.

An agent saves money only on the calls it resolves from start to finish. In this section's model of a contact center, each extra point of resolution is worth far more than a cheaper token.

Every in-scope call in the model reaches the agent first. Calls it cannot resolve go to a person, and those tend to be the longer, harder ones; some calls it closes come back as repeat calls. The defaults use published per-minute pricing and a fully loaded human cost per call, with platform, integration and team costs as one fixed annual line. The slider to watch is the resolution rate: the note under the chart converts each 5 points of it into dollars a month.

On the default assumptions, savings begin at about 40 percent resolution and grow with every point above it. The model is illustrative only. Author's arithmetic.

The escalation and repeat-call defaults are my assumptions, not sourced figures, and a team should use its own. The tool's worst case sets resolution 15 points lower, escalations 15 points costlier and repeat calls 5 points higher. Its best case sets resolution 10 points higher, escalations 10 points cheaper and repeat calls 3 points lower.

A published comparison points the same way. The pricier stack with higher resolution, at 14 cents a minute and 70 percent resolved, costs less per 1,000 calls than the cheaper one, at 9 cents a minute and 55 percent. Cost models that count only LLM token savings miss most of the money. The next section turns from what an agent is worth to how it should be built.
