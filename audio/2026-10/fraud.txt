Section 8: Trust and identity.

A voice is no longer proof of identity.

Voices can now be cloned, so an identity check has to rise with the harm that a wrong answer could do.

Pindrop, a deepfake-detection vendor, reports that over half of fraud attempts at health care contact centers now involve AI-generated elements. It counts synthetic voice, bots and reconnaissance through the phone menu (the interactive voice response system, or IVR) among them. Vendor-reported.

Identity on the phone is a brand question as much as a security one. Customers will be targeted by callers pretending to be the organization, and its agent will hear callers pretending to be customers. A voiceprint alone does not settle who is speaking: in 2023 a reporter got into his own bank account with an AI clone of his voice.

Heavier checks have costs too. Liveness checks, which test for a live speaker, reject some genuine callers over phone-quality audio, and texted codes lose some older or distressed callers. The six controls below match the friction to the risk and keep a person as the fallback. Author's view.

Six controls put the strongest checks on the requests that can do the most harm. They are my recommendations.

Run liveness and deepfake detection in the contact center platform, before any protected data is disclosed.

Step up verification with a one-time code to the number on file; a birth date or security questions alone are not enough.

For payments, prescription changes and other authorizations, add voiceprint verification with liveness detection.

Match the friction to the risk: passive signals for status checks, a one-time code for account changes, and voiceprint plus liveness only for irreversible actions. Always offer a person as the fallback.

Publish a "we will never ask you for" policy, and make marketing its owner.

In the US, outbound AI-voice calls have counted as artificial-voice calls under the Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA) since a 2024 ruling by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC), so they generally need prior express consent. Health care exemptions are narrow, so reminder programs should be checked with counsel. Consent belongs to the current subscriber, so outbound lists should be checked against the FCC Reassigned Numbers Database before every campaign.
