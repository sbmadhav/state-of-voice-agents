T6. Limited Memory Language Models.

How LMLM works, and what it has shown.

Zhao et al. "Pre-training Limited Memory Language Models with Internal and External Knowledge," ICLR 2026. Code and 176-million-parameter and 382 million checkpoints are public under MIT.

Mechanism.

Training text is annotated with inline lookups: entity, relation, then the retrieved value.

Loss is zeroed on the retrieved value tokens. The model still trains on every other token, so it learns when and how to look up, not the value itself.

The store holds 54.6 million (entity, relation, value) triples extracted from Wikipedia by an 8 billion annotator trained on GPT-4o-labeled seeds.

At inference, a lookup token pauses generation; the query is matched by embedding similarity (rejection threshold 0.6) and the value is inserted.

Budget: about 3 billion tokens, 8 epochs, at most 8 H100-days per model.

Follow-up: Co-LMLM (July 2026).

Replaces relational triples with vector keys and free-text values, so knowledge is no longer limited to entity attributes.

About 2.2 milliseconds per retrieval versus 28.6 milliseconds for a question-generating lookup, which fits inside a voice turn.

Reports that lookup timing is imperfect: forcing lookups added 5.9 points on SimpleQA.

LLaMA2 standard. Params: 382 million. FactScore: 14.0. T-REx EM: 52.0. PopQA: 22.7.

LLaMA2 LMLM. Params: 382 million. FactScore: 31.9. T-REx EM: 58.1. PopQA: 50.8.

LLaMA2 LMLM, store off. Params: 382 million. FactScore: 12.8. T-REx EM: 38.5. PopQA: n/a.

LLaMA2. Params: 7 billion. FactScore: 34.0. T-REx EM: 60.5. PopQA: 29.2.

LLaMA3.1. Params: 8 billion. FactScore: 40.3. T-REx EM: 67.3. PopQA: 29.4.

Source: Zhao et al. A 355 million RAG baseline scored higher on T-REx than the same-size LMLM (75.8 versus 58.7) but lower on FactScore and PopQA, so retrieval-augmented frontier models remain the practical benchmark to beat.

How much of a model is fact storage?

LMLM 382 million ≈ LLaMA2-7B18x.

RETRO 7.5 billion ≈ GPT-3 175B25x.

Atlas 11 billion > 540 billion on NQ50x.

Parameter reduction at comparable performance on knowledge-heavy tasks. Models store about 2 bits of knowledge per parameter, falling toward 1 bit for rarely seen facts. Question-answering accuracy tracks how often a fact appears in training data. None of these measure the share of a frontier model's weights spent on facts; reasoning, code and multilingual skill need parameters too.

Every fact it shares is correct; it cannot hallucinate. Verdict: overstated. Defensible version: It cuts hallucination sharply and makes each fact traceable; errors from wrong lookups, missing rows and extraction remain.

It keeps non-repeating data out of the model. Verdict: partly. Defensible version: It offloads factual attributes by type, common and rare alike; rare facts are where models learn worst.

Fact storage is ~99 percent of model bloat. Verdict: unsupported. Defensible version: Retrieval models match models 18 to 50 times larger on knowledge tasks; the exact share is unmeasured.

It makes training cheap. Verdict: partly. Defensible version: It still trains on the full corpus and adds lookup and store-building cost; the saving is fewer parameters for a given factual accuracy, which lowers serving cost.

It keeps sensitive data out of weights. Verdict: plausible, untested. Defensible version: Designed to, and extraction attacks show why it matters, but no privacy-attack evaluation has been published.

What has to be true for regulated contact centers.

Extraction quality: turning CRM, EHR and policy text into rows relies on LLM annotators; their errors become confident wrong answers.

Beyond entity facts: contact center knowledge is often procedural ("if the plan is X and the claim is Y"). Free-text values help, unproven here.

Reliable lookups: the model must know when to look up and must not fall back on weights.

Live data still needs tools: balances and appointments change by the minute; LMLM covers static knowledge, not transactions.

Total cost versus RAG: pre-training a private small model plus building and refreshing the store must beat retrieval on a frontier API.

Evidence at scale: nothing yet on dialog, speech, models above about 400 million, or enterprise data. A 2026 position paper argues for this direction but reports no deployments.
