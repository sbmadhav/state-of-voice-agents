T9. Regulation.

The rules ask mostly for disclosure and consent, and they are tightening.

Most of the US rules that touch a health care voice agent ask it to disclose that it is an AI or to obtain consent, and new state laws took effect in 2025 and 2026.

The table summarizes selected US rules for orientation. It is not legal advice and not a complete list. Its focus is US health care; financial services adds its own regimes, not reviewed here: the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (GLBA), the card industry's data security standard (PCI DSS) and the rules on unfair, deceptive or abusive acts or practices (UDAAP). Items marked unverified come from secondary sources and need confirmation against the enacted text and by counsel.

Most of the ten rules ask for disclosure or consent, and the newest carry dates in 2026 and 2027. Two statuses are marked unverified, and all of it is orientation, not legal advice.

Telephone Consumer Protection Act (TCPA), FCC 24-17. AI voices count as "artificial," so outbound calls generally need prior express consent, with narrow health care exemptions. Consent belongs to the current subscriber, so call lists need checking against the Reassigned Numbers Database. The cost is 500 dollars to 1,500 dollars per violation. It has been in force since February 8, 2024.

Call recording consent (state wiretap laws). Some states, including California, Florida, Maryland and Pennsylvania, require every party's consent to record, so the agent should announce recording and transcription at the start of the call. These laws are in force and vary by state, so they need checking with counsel.

HIPAA BAAs. Every layer that touches protected health information (PHI) needs one: the model, router, speech-to-text, text-to-speech, orchestration, telephony and testing. Major model providers offer them, with retention options.

California AB 3030. Clinical messages from generative AI need a disclaimer and a route to a human, unless a clinician has reviewed them. It took effect on January 1, 2025.

California AB 489. AI may not imply that it holds a health care license. It took effect on January 1, 2026.

California AB 1609. Large businesses must disclose chatbots and offer a human within set times. It was reported signed on September 28, 2026. Unverified.

Texas TRAIGA (HB 149). Health care providers must disclose AI use to patients. It took effect on January 1, 2026.

Utah AI Policy Act. Regulated occupations must make the disclosure proactively in high-risk interactions. It is in force.

Colorado SB 26-189. It replaces the 2024 AI Act, which never took effect. It covers automated decisions in consequential areas such as credit, employment and housing, and carries no general chatbot disclosure duty. It is reported to take effect on January 1, 2027. Unverified.

Right to deletion (state privacy laws, GDPR). Personal data must be erased on request. This is easier to honor in the model when facts live in a store, not weights. Logs, transcripts and backups still need their own deletion.

None of this replaces counsel. The table does show where the rules concentrate: disclosure of AI use, consent to outbound calls and to recording, and a route to a human. The next section checks claims made about the technology itself.
