T9. Regulation.

Mostly disclosure and consent, and tightening.

A summary of selected US rules for orientation, not legal advice and not a complete list. US healthcare focus; financial services adds its own regimes (GLBA, PCI DSS, UDAAP) not reviewed here. Items marked unverified come from secondary sources and need confirmation against the enacted text and by counsel.

TCPA, FCC 24-17. What it requires: AI voices count as "artificial"; outbound calls generally need prior express consent, with narrow healthcare exemptions; consent belongs to the current subscriber, so check the Reassigned Numbers Database; 500 dollars to 1,500 dollars per violation. Status: In force since February 8, 2024.

Call recording consent (state wiretap laws). What it requires: Some states, including California, Florida, Maryland and Pennsylvania, require every party's consent to record; announce recording and transcription at the start of the call. Status: In force; varies by state; check with counsel.

HIPAA BAAs. What it requires: Every layer that touches PHI: model, router, STT, TTS, orchestration, telephony, testing. Status: Available from major model providers with retention options.

California AB 3030. What it requires: Disclaimer and a route to a human on generative AI clinical messages, unless clinician-reviewed. Status: Effective January 1, 2025.

California AB 489. What it requires: AI may not imply it holds a health care license. Status: Effective January 1, 2026.

California AB 1609. What it requires: Large businesses disclose chatbots and offer a human within set times. Status: Reported signed September 28, 2026, unverified.

Texas TRAIGA (HB 149). What it requires: Health care providers disclose AI use to patients. Status: Effective January 1, 2026.

Utah AI Policy Act. What it requires: Proactive disclosure in high-risk interactions by regulated occupations. Status: In force.

Colorado SB 26-189. What it requires: Replaces the 2024 AI Act, which never took effect; covers automated decisions in consequential areas such as credit, employment and housing, with no general chatbot disclosure duty. Status: Reported effective January 1, 2027, unverified.

Right to deletion (state privacy laws, GDPR). What it requires: Erase personal data on request. Status: Easier to honor in the model when facts live in a store, not weights; logs, transcripts and backups still need their own deletion.
