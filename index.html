How to read this update: method, labels and disclosure
  • Delta only. Findings published between October 12 and November 14, 2026 carry a new tag. Older material appears only if October did not cover it (most of the financial services evidence). Where a figure changed, the October value is shown next to it.
  • Labels. independent vendor-reported company-reported author's view. Every figure links to its source.
  • Method. A review of public sources up to the research cutoff. I did not run my own tests.
  • Terms. "Repeat reliability" (pass^3) is the share of test scenarios passed on all three tries. WER is the share of words transcribed wrongly. Full-duplex models listen and talk at the same time. A System-1 model makes fast typed decisions (route, risk, done talking) without writing prose.

3 · New failure mode

The TV in the room

October listed background chatter as one failure among many (T4). New evidence puts it first.

The noise that hurts most is not traffic or wind. It is another voice. Veris called 14 voice systems as a simulated utility-billing customer, 100 tasks run three times each (1,906 calls scored), with street, café and TV audio mixed in at equal loudness. With the TV on, task completion fell from 38.7% (372 of 961 calls) to 8.6% (81 of 945). Nine of the 14 systems fell below 5%; only GPT-Live 1 (36.1%) and Grok Voice (33.8%) stayed above 20%. Caller verification fell from 81.3% to 52.5%, and 90% of TV calls carried 10 or more words the caller never said, against 2% of clean calls.[4] new vendor-reported Vendors' noise options were left off and the calls were simulated, so treat the levels as directional.

Controls
  1. Isolate the caller's voice before speech-to-text and turn detection.
  2. Let a voice interrupt the agent only when it matches the caller.
  3. Read back identity details, amounts and account numbers before any write; re-verify if a different voice answers a security question.
  4. Add TV, café and second-speaker audio to the golden test set and block releases on it.

6 · Fraud and identity

Correction: voice is a signal, not a factor

October's tiers (T8) put voiceprint plus liveness at the top. That missed guidance already in place, and new research reinforces the change.

What changed
  • Correction: New York's financial regulator advised in October 2024 against voice as an authentication factor, preferring liveness checks or phishing-resistant methods.[13] independent
  • New: deepfake detectors degrade substantially after the audio crosses a real phone line. On call audio, one detector's equal error rate (where missed fakes equal false alarms) was 37.9%, and 22.4% after retraining on call audio.[14] new independent
  • Revised tier: for payments, payee changes and limit increases, use an app push or another phishing-resistant check; keep voiceprint and liveness as risk signals that can block, never as the only factor that approves. author's view
Financial fraud data
  • Synthetic-voice attacks rose 149% at banks and 475% at insurers in 2024, across 1.2 billion calls analyzed.[53] vendor-reported
  • One credit union's deepfake detection avoided $2.57M of fraud exposure in about 13 months.[54] vendor-reported
  • US consumers reported $3.5B lost to impostor scams in 2025; bank impersonators accounted for nearly $1B, more than government impersonators.[55] independent
  • Generative AI could push US fraud losses from $12.3B in 2023 to $40B by 2027 (a projection).[56] independent

7 · Models, platforms and routing

What shipped, and what it changes

Releases that change a recommendation, then a short list of everything else. The architecture and routing analysis is in October, T1 and T5.

Fast decision models (System 1)
  • OpenAI Decisions API: beta to generally available (Oct 22) on GPT-6 Luna, up to 400 questions a request, $0.10 per million input tokens. GPT-Live sends turns to it while the caller is still speaking. Sierra, a customer, reported p95 under 280 ms in launch coverage.[15][57] new vendor-reported
  • New options: Zoom Decisions ($0.042 and $0.10 per million input tokens; 81.3% and 83.9% on five text benchmarks)[58]; LiveKit's DecisionModel runs named decisions beside the call.[59] new vendor-reported
  • Jev: October p50 175 ms, p95 270 ms; a second test measured p50 151 ms, p95 247 ms. No self-hosted version exists.[60][61] independent
  • Edge: open Laya needs about 1 GB (0.85 GB ONNX at fp16); a Cloudflare Worker gets 128 MB. Run decision models in containers or on your own servers.[62][63] author's view
Speech, telephony and contact center
  • ElevenLabs: Eleven Realtime Max scored 98.8% on Big Bench Audio reasoning and 87.1% on tool-use task success in independent tests, with 0.57 s to first audio, slower than the 470 ms claimed; it opened to all workspaces on Nov 2.[64][65][31] independent Its agents now plug natively into NiCE CXone, Genesys Cloud, Amazon Connect and Five9, so a pilot can sit inside an existing contact center.[66] new vendor-reported HIPAA and retention terms for Realtime are not yet documented.
  • Deepgram: Think-Live, which decides when the agent should listen, wait or respond, is generally available, and Flux TTS is GA in nine languages (Oct 29). No benchmarks published.[67] new vendor-reported
  • OpenAI: GPT-Live connects directly to phone lines over SIP (the standard for business telephony), inbound and outbound, with call transfer.[68] vendor-reported
  • Two new models are too slow for live voice: in Regal's Oct 15 run, GPT-6.1 Sol (1,711 ms) and Gemini 3.8 Flash (2,516 ms) miss its 1,300 ms bar; GPT-6 Sol leads at 786 of 1,000.[69] new vendor-reported
  • Open speech: NVIDIA Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe (4.71% English WER, commercial license) is for recorded calls; it does not stream.[16][70] Gradium Phonon produces speech in about 43 ms on a server CPU and about 7 ms on a laptop CPU; only its 31M English model is Apache-2.0, while the 40M and multilingual models are non-commercial.[17][71] new vendor-reported
  • Market: Salesforce agreed to acquire Cresta (Nov 2) and Five9 to acquire Level AI for about $325M (Nov 5); check data-export and exit terms in any new contract.[72][73] new company-reported
Also shipped, no change to recommendations
  • Anthropic Claude Fable 5.2 (Nov 9): a text model, usable as the reasoner behind a voice layer; $8 and $40 per million input and output tokens, up to 30% faster than 5.1; no audio and no voice-agent benchmarks yet.[74] new vendor-reported
  • Phonic-1 (Oct 29): a speech-to-speech model claiming 83.2 on tau-cubed voice (Sierra's benchmark of voice agents completing customer-service tasks) against 79.9 for GPT-Live 1.[75] new vendor-reported
  • NVIDIA NemotronLabs VoiceChat (Sept 2026): an open full-duplex model that calls tools while it talks; 33.0% pass@1 on tool use against 54.0% for Gemini 3.1 Live.[76] vendor-reported
  • Meta: voice conversations in its consumer Muse app (US, Nov 5); no developer API or enterprise voice model.[77] new vendor-reported
  • Frameworks: Pipecat pairs a fast front-end model with a slower back-end model; Deepgram is acquiring Daily, Pipecat's maker, and Pipecat stays open source.[78][79] new vendor-reported
  • Research: ConvFill (a small talker covering a frontier model's delay; a later paper notes it does not measure the risk of the filler contradicting the final answer)[80][81] independent; Block's Dessa (full-duplex, trained only on audio, no benchmarks).[82] new

9 · Health care notes

What changed for health systems

October's health care analysis stands: published results, readiness by call type and regulation.

  • California AB 1979 (signed Oct 2026, effective Jan 1, 2027): a licensed provider must exercise independent judgment when AI output informs care, AI may not perform reserved clinical functions, and health care chatbot operators are treated as providers under the state's medical privacy law. Scheduling and routine messages are exempt.[18] new independent
  • Payer and administrator calls: a health plan administrator is putting voice agents on inbound provider calls about eligibility, benefits, balances and claims status, handing off to people in its existing contact center (Nov 4).[83] new vendor-reported
  • Clinic results: about 50% of calls handled end to end in a vendor's most recently onboarded pilot clinics.[84] new vendor-reported
  • Clinical speech: Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe's error rate on accented clinical speech fell from 30.8% to 23.8% with prompting alone, and to 11.8% after fine-tuning; test on your own patients' audio.[16] new vendor-reported
  • The TV-in-the-room finding applies equally to patients calling from home or a waiting room. author's view
