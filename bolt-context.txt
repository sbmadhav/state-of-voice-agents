3 · NEW FAILURE MODE

The TV in the room

October listed background chatter as one failure among many (T4). New evidence puts it first.

The noise that hurts most is not traffic or wind. It is another voice. Veris called 14 voice systems as a simulated utility-billing customer, 100 tasks run three times each (1,906 calls scored), with street, café and TV audio mixed in at equal loudness. With the TV on, task completion fell from 38.7% (372 of 961 calls) to 8.6% (81 of 945). Nine of the 14 systems fell below 5%; only GPT-Live 1 (36.1%) and Grok Voice (33.8%) stayed above 20%. Caller verification fell from 81.3% to 52.5%, and 90% of TV calls carried 10 or more words the caller never said, against 2% of clean calls.[4] NEW vendor-reported Vendors' noise options were left off and the calls were simulated, so treat the levels as directional.

CONTROLS
Isolate the caller's voice before speech-to-text and turn detection.
Let a voice interrupt the agent only when it matches the caller.

6 · FRAUD AND IDENTITY

Correction: voice is a signal, not a factor

October's tiers (T8) put voiceprint plus liveness at the top. That missed guidance already in place, and new research reinforces the change.

WHAT CHANGED
Correction: New York's financial regulator advised in October 2024 against voice as an authentication factor, preferring liveness checks or phishing-resistant methods.[13] independent
New: deepfake detectors degrade substantially after the audio crosses a real phone line. On call audio, one detector's equal error rate (where missed fakes equal false alarms) was 37.9%, and 22.4% after retraining on call audio.[14] NEW independent
Revised tier: for payments, payee changes and limit increases, use an app push or another phishing-resistant check; keep voiceprint and liveness as risk signals that can block, never as the only factor that approves. author's view
FINANCIAL FRAUD DATA
Synthetic-voice attacks rose 149% at banks and 475% at insurers in 2024, across 1.2 billion calls analyzed.[53] vendor-reported
One credit union's deepfake detection avoided $2.57M of fraud exposure in about 13 months.[54] vendor-reported
US consumers reported $3.5B lost to impostor scams in 2025; bank impersonators accounted for nearly $1B, more than government impersonators.[55] independent
Generative AI could push US fraud losses from $12.3B in 2023 to $40B by 2027 (a projection).[56] independent

9 · HEALTH CARE NOTES

What changed for health systems

October's health care analysis stands: published results, readiness by call type and regulation.

California AB 1979 (signed Oct 2026, effective Jan 1, 2027): a licensed provider must exercise independent judgment when AI output informs care, AI may not perform reserved clinical functions, and health care chatbot operators are treated as providers under the state's medical privacy law. Scheduling and routine messages are exempt.[18] NEW independent
Payer and administrator calls: a health plan administrator is putting voice agents on inbound provider calls about eligibility, benefits, balances and claims status, handing off to people in its existing contact center (Nov 4).[83] NEW vendor-reported
Clinic results: about 50% of calls handled end to end in a vendor's most recently onboarded pilot clinics.[84] NEW vendor-reported
Clinical speech: Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe's error rate on accented clinical speech fell from 30.8% to 23.8% with prompting alone, and to 11.8% after fine-tuning; test on your own patients' audio.[16] NEW vendor-reported

STATE OF CONVERSATIONAL VOICE AGENTS · HEALTHCARE FIRST, WITH NOTES FOR FINANCIAL SERVICES

October 2026 edition

Voice agents at the front door

Voice agents are now good enough to take routine calls in regulated contact centers: scheduling, billing questions, balance and status checks. They are not yet consistent enough to run unsupervised on clinical or high-risk financial conversations. In one vendor-run benchmark, the leading platform still failed about one in four test scenarios when each was run three times.#

THE ONE-MINUTE READ

What we know. The best platform passed 75.6% of test scenarios on all three tries; the top three are within the margin of error. Every published deployment result is administrative and self-reported.

What to watch. Demos overstate readiness, cloned voices defeat voice-only identity checks, and scores move so fast that any model bet goes stale in a quarter.

What to do. Build a golden test set from your own calls and gate every change on it. Pilot narrow, judged on cost per resolved call. Separate silent decisions from the conversation, and build a governed fact store; trial limited-memory models only once they beat retrieval.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY · ABOUT TEN MINUTES

What this report argues, in plain terms

Voice agents can now take routine calls in regulated contact centers. What separates the teams that succeed is not which model they buy, but how they test, how the agent decides, what it remembers, who it is listening to, and how it proves who the caller is. Nine points, each linked to the evidence.

1
Ready for routine calls, not for unsupervised high-risk ones

Agents now handle scheduling, billing questions and status checks well. In one public benchmark, the best platform completed over 90% of scenarios on a single try, but passed only 75.6% when each scenario had to succeed three times in a row.[2] The gap is consistency, not capability. Health systems report large gains, such as waits cut from 6.2 to 2.4 minutes and 44% of repetitive calls automated, but every published result is administrative and self-reported.[5][6]

Reliability and results
2
Evaluation is the asset that lasts

Models change every month; the top score on one voice benchmark rose from 30% to 67% in eight months.[7] What you keep is a golden test set: hundreds of real, de-identified call scenarios with known right answers, including noisy lines and upset callers, run three times on every change. A drop in score blocks the release, the way failing tests block code. Build it before choosing a vendor: it is how you compare vendors, gate releases and hold contracts to account.

The golden test set
3
Think silently, then respond

People size up a question before they answer it. A voice agent should too. A small, fast "System 1" model answers typed questions in tens of milliseconds, each with a confidence score: which team owns this, is there fraud or clinical risk, is the caller finished, is it even the caller speaking. It never writes prose. A conversational LLM then responds, with the right data already fetched.

This is faster and cheaper: in one test, routing first kept accuracy at about 84% while cutting cost from $2.42 to about $0.69 per 1,000 requests.[8] It is also safer, because every decision is a logged record, and fraud, clinical and complaint signals always go to a person by rule. It works beside any voice stack, and it can cut the audio mid-sentence if it spots risk.

The thinker-responder model
4
Hear the right caller

Real calls come from cars, kitchens and waiting rooms. In one vendor test, background speech pushed transcription errors to 22% to 31%, and isolating the main speaker cut them by up to 43%.[9] Diarization separates the voices, so the agent locks onto the caller and checks identity again if a different voice takes over before a sensitive step. Test with noise and side conversations, not clean demo audio.

Noise and diarization
5
Emotion and persona: worth piloting, not yet proven

Detecting frustration in real time lets the agent slow down or hand off to a person, and scoring sentiment on every call replaces quality reviews that sample a few percent. A persona is the brand's voice: offer callers a choice, then keep each caller's preferred voice consistent. The evidence is still mostly vendor case studies, and the EU requires telling people when emotion recognition is used,[10] so run both as experiments behind the golden test set.

Emotion and persona
6
Build governed memory now; train limited-memory models later

Turn plans, policies, fees and provider details into a governed fact store: versioned rows with owners, access control and deletion. It powers retrieval today. Limited-memory models go further: they are trained to look facts up in that store instead of memorizing them. In research, a 382M-parameter model approached a 7B model on factual precision, and deleting a row made the model forget the fact.[11] That research is early (Wikipedia facts, small models, no voice or production use), so trial it on one domain once the store is ready, and scale only if it beats retrieval. Live data such as balances and appointment slots always comes from your systems through tools.

Governed memory
7
A voice is no longer proof of identity

Over half of fraud attempts at healthcare contact centers now involve AI-generated elements such as synthetic voice.[4] Match the check to the risk: passive signals for status checks, a one-time code for account changes, voiceprint plus liveness detection for irreversible actions, and always a person as the fallback. Card numbers should never reach the agent at all. For outbound calls, consent belongs to the current subscriber, so check lists against the FCC's Reassigned Numbers Database.[12]

Trust and identity
8
The money is in resolution, not token price

Savings come from calls the agent truly resolves, meaning no repeat contact on any channel within 48 hours. The calls left for people are the harder ones, so honest models show smaller savings than vendor pitches. With this report's default assumptions, the agent breaks even at about 40% resolution, and each extra point of resolution is worth far more than cheaper tokens. Contract on price per resolved call, with rights to export logs and evaluation data.

The economics model
9
What to do in the next 90 days

Pick two or three inbound administrative call types in one market. Build the golden test set (200+ scenarios). Set the go-live bar by risk: about 85% repeat reliability for informational calls, 95% for anything that writes data or moves money, and zero critical failures. Transfer to a person with full context. At day 90, judge on cost per resolved call, abandonment and zero wrong-account actions. Whatever the outcome, keep the test set, routing logs and recordings: they are the most valuable thing the pilot produces.

5 · THINK SILENTLY, THEN RESPOND

A thinker-responder model for conversational agents

People don't say every thought out loud before they answer. We size up the question, decide what it's about, recall what we need, and then speak. A voice agent should work the same way: a fast System-1 model is the thinker, making typed decisions silently, and a conversational LLM is the responder, turning those decisions into a natural reply.[22][20][19]

ONE CALLER TURN, TWO WAYS
Caller: "I got charged twice for my visit last month."

The caller hears one direct answer. The decisions are logged as structured data, not spoken.

WHAT THE THINKER DECIDES (SYSTEM 1, SILENT, TYPED)
Intent: which sub-agent owns this turn (billing, scheduling, complaint)
Risk: fraud, clinical, vulnerable-caller or complaint flags that force a handoff
Tools: which MCP servers to expose, and which facts to fetch before speaking
Turn state: is the caller done, correcting themselves, or just saying "mm-hmm"
Speaker: is this the caller, or someone else in the room
Emotion: frustration or confusion that should change the tone, slow things down or hand off
Confidence: a probability on each, so low-confidence turns hand off instead of guessing
WHY IT WORKS
Faster: decisions take tens to hundreds of milliseconds, so the responder starts with data already fetched.
Cheaper: the expensive model only writes the reply, not the routing.
Safer: the "thought" is a structured record, not prose, so it can be logged, audited and gated by rules. The responder can't narrate a tool result it never received.[23]
A deliberate difference: OpenAI and Sierra put the fast model on the talking side and a slower reasoner behind it.[26][14] This design puts the fast model on the deciding side. The two combine: a fast talker, a fast silent decider, and a slower reasoner when needed.
Works with any voice stack: the thinker does not need a speech-to-text pipeline. Beside a speech-to-speech or full-duplex model it reads the transcript stream and decides which tools and policies apply, so the voice model keeps its natural turn-taking. Because a full-duplex model starts speaking within a few hundred milliseconds, the thinker also needs a stop control: when it flags risk mid-turn, it cuts the audio output at once, before protected data or advice reaches the caller.
Limit: the thinker handles bounded choices. Open-ended reasoning still needs a deeper planner the responder can call.

6 · WHAT SEPARATES A GOOD AGENT

Two foundations, and two experiments worth running

Models change every month. Two capabilities decide whether an agent holds up on real calls, and they carry over from one model or vendor to the next. Two more are promising but early: pilot them behind the golden test set.

FOUNDATION · A GOLDEN TEST SET AND AN EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

The most valuable asset a team builds. Hundreds of real, de-identified call scenarios, each with a known right answer, run three times on every change to models, prompts, tools or personas.

Common guidance: 200+ scenarios before production, 500+ for multilingual enterprise use, [27] vendor-reported To detect a 5-point change in a pass rate near 80%, plan for several hundred runs per version, not the 100 sometimes quoted. author's arithmetic
Mix happy paths with edge cases, errors, adversarial callers and noisy audio, and add every production failure as a new case.
Block a release when a score drops, the same way failing tests block code.
FOUNDATION · HEARING THE RIGHT CALLER

Real callers phone from cars, kitchens and waiting rooms, with a TV on or someone talking beside them. How the agent performs there matters more than how it performs in a quiet demo.

Background speech pushed streaming speech-to-text error rates to 22% to 31%; isolating the main speaker cut errors by up to 43%.[9] vendor-reported
Separate the voices (diarization), lock onto the caller, and re-verify identity if a different voice takes over before a sensitive step.[28]
Test at several noise levels and with side conversations, not just clean audio.
EXPERIMENT · EMOTION IN REAL TIME, SENTIMENT ON EVERY CALL

During the call, frustration or confusion should change how the agent speaks or trigger a warm handoff. After the call, sentiment scored on every call replaces quality reviews that sample a few percent.

Treat emotion scores as a signal for routing and review, not a judgment of the caller. Accuracy varies, so check that they work equally across accents and age groups.
In the EU, people must be told when an emotion recognition system is used.[10]
EXPERIMENT · PERSONA IS THE BRAND

Callers judge the organization by how its agent sounds. Voice, pace, warmth and word choice deserve the same care as a brand guide.

One subscription business reported resolution rates up more than 50% after tuning its voice persona.[14] vendor-reported
Offer callers a choice of a few personas, then keep each caller's preferred voice consistent on every call. author's view
Every persona says it is an AI, never implies a professional license, and gives the same facts and policies.[29]

author's view The experiments have little evidence so far: mostly vendor case studies, and none yet shows emotion detection or personas improving AI-agent outcomes at scale.

9 · WHAT COMES NEXT

Models that look facts up instead of memorizing them

A Limited Memory Language Model (LMLM) is trained so that specific facts, such as a plan's coverage or a card's limit, live in an external database, not in the model's weights. During training the model is not rewarded for memorizing those values; it learns to issue a lookup and copy the answer. Language and reasoning stay in the model. Facts stay in a table people can inspect, correct and delete.[11] It suits slow-changing knowledge such as benefits, policies and fees; live data such as balances, claims and appointment slots stays on tool and MCP calls.

WHY A GOVERNED FACT STORE FITS REGULATED CONVERSATIONS
Facts become governed data. Each one is a row with access control, versioning and change review.
Deletion from the model is real. Removing rows forgets them without retraining.[11] Copies in logs, transcripts and backups need their own retention rules.
Less sensitive data in weights. Researchers have extracted names, phone numbers and emails from model weights, even from data seen once.[33][34]
Every answer has provenance. Each lookup is a logged call with the value it returned.
Room to shrink the model. In research, a 382M limited-memory model came close to a 7B model on factual precision; details are in the technical appendix (T6).[11]
MY VIEW: BUILD THE FACT STORE NOW, AND TREAT LIMITED-MEMORY TRAINING AS A GATED BET

Retrieval on a frontier model is the safe default today, and the evidence for limited-memory models is still research-stage: Wikipedia facts, models under 400M parameters, no dialog, voice or production use. A governed fact store pays off either way. Training is a bet to stage behind gates, not a commitment.

Now · no-regret
Build the governed fact store

Turn plans, policies, products, providers and fees into versioned rows with owners, access control and deletion. It powers retrieval today and LMLM tomorrow.

When the store is ready · trial
Trial a small model on one domain

Start from the open LMLM code (MIT) on one de-identified domain, such as benefits or product FAQs. Go further only if it beats retrieval on factual precision, deletion, latency and total cost.[35]

Only if it wins · scale
Put it behind the responder

Expand domains for static knowledge. Live balances, claims and appointment slots always stay on tools.

11 · A PILOT PLAYBOOK

A 90-day pilot with gates set before launch

Teams are at different points on this path. If you are starting out, expanding to new call types or resetting a stalled rollout, this is one way to run it; if you are already in production, the golden test set, gates and kill criteria work as an audit of what you have. The shape: one market or line of business, inbound only, two or three call types, warm transfer with full context. The pre-launch bar is set above the best public benchmark on purpose, because public scenarios are generic.

Weeks 0 to 4
Build the golden test set

200+ scenarios from your own de-identified calls: accents, older callers, Spanish, number dictation, mid-call corrections, background noise and chatter, upset callers. Silent shadow test on recorded audio.

Gate · end of week 4
Pass^3 bar set by risk

About 85% for informational calls, 95% for anything that writes data or moves money, and zero critical failures at any rate. Every scenario run three times on phone-grade audio. Router thresholds calibrated on held-out data. Identity controls signed off.

Weeks 5 to 12
Live in one market

Every failure becomes a regression test. Weekly audit of tool-call write-backs and misroutes (transfers after routing).

Day 90
Scale, adjust, or stop

Decide against the criteria below, not against a demo.

SCALE IF
Success at day 90
Abandonment down 30% or more on in-scope queues
50% or more of in-scope calls resolved without a person, with no repeat contact on any channel within 48 hours (hang-ups don't count)
Zero wrong-customer or wrong-account write-backs on audit
Satisfaction at or above the human baseline
Recording, transcript, routing decision and tool trace on 100% of calls
STOP IF
Kill criteria, any time
Any protected data disclosed to an unverified caller
Any advice beyond approved content: clinical guidance, or a financial recommendation outside an approved script
Wrong-action rate above 0.5% of tool calls on audit
A vendor refuses to export logs or evaluation data

author's view Starting points tied to the published results above, not an industry standard. The pre-launch bar is a risk-management choice, not a benchmark-derived number: a narrow golden set of two or three call types is easier than a broad generic benchmark, so it can sit above the best public score (75.6%), and the bar rises with the harm a failure can do. A high overall pass rate never offsets a critical failure, such as protected data given to the wrong caller or a wrong payment; one of those blocks launch. With 200 scenarios, a measured 90% carries a margin of about plus or minus 4 points. 30% lower abandonment is about half what peers report (56% to 85%). 50% resolution is in the range of reported automation (44% at one health system). Tighten the bar for higher-risk call types, and lower the resolution target for complex queues.

Contact Center Operations

Start with scheduling, billing and status checks; warm transfer with full context.

Evidence: Intermountain: 44% of repetitive calls automated and 85% lower abandonment. Concern: Wrong write-backs, misroutes, authentication failures, staff morale. Would change their mind: Misroute or wrong-action rate above 0.5% on audit.

Clinical & Risk Safety Lead

Administrative only. No triage, no advice. Escalation by rule, not by threshold.

Evidence: No validated autonomous triage deployment found. Best models reach only about 53% on multi-intent benchmarks. Concern: Missed red flags, older and non-English callers, regulatory exposure. Would change their mind: An independent safety evaluation with prospective outcomes.

AI Platform Architect

Thinker-responder: tiered router in front, narrowed tools per sub-agent, and a governed fact store, with a limited-memory trial for static knowledge once it beats retrieval. On a managed platform such as Sierra, insist on exporting routing decisions and logs.

Evidence: A 0.1B fine-tuned classifier hits 93.5% on Banking77 in 22 ms. Tool retrieval more than tripled selection accuracy. A 382M LMLM approached a 7B model on factual precision. Concern: Lock-in at orchestration and integration layers; model churn every few weeks. Would change their mind: A vendor refusing to export logs or routing decisions.

Skeptic / red team

Do not trust vendor benchmarks or one-lab results. Demand your own repeat-reliability numbers.

Evidence: Jev's Banking77 score varies 10.7 points by lab. Benign context flipped 61% of correct decisions. LMLM is untested above 400M parameters or on dialog. Sierra publishes single-run voice scores, not repeat reliability. Concern: Every outcome figure here is customer- or vendor-reported, with no control group. Would change their mind: 90%+ pass^3 on your own scenarios, audited by an independent party.

WHERE THE PERSPECTIVES CONVERGE
Start with inbound administrative calls and warm human transfer.
Build a golden evaluation set before choosing a vendor, and gate every change on it.
Measure repeat reliability and cost per resolved call, not containment or demo quality.
Use a fast router, with calibrated thresholds and rule-based escalation.
Own the evaluation data, routing logs and call logs, whichever vendor wins.
STILL UNRESOLVED
Fine-tuned classifier (cheapest, most accurate on fixed intents) versus a hosted decision API (no training data, adapts to new intents).
When a private limited-memory model beats retrieval on a frontier model, on total cost.
Speed versus safety on outbound calls, where consent rules bite at scale.

T2 · LATENCY BUDGET

Where the seconds go in a turn

Human turn gaps across ten languages cluster around a quarter second.[40] Measured platform turns in the benchmark averaged 1.27 s to 3.08 s.[2] Adjust the stages, or pick a preset.

Be candid about the trade-off: a cascade (speech-to-text, LLM, text-to-speech) takes turns like a walkie-talkie. Even with a fast router and prefetch it lands near 1.5 s a turn, and cutting endpointing further clips callers mid-sentence. If turns under 800 ms are a hard requirement, use a speech-to-speech or full-duplex model and accept a coarser, after-the-fact audit trail. The thinker fits beside either.

SAME MODEL, DIFFERENT HARNESS

In Cekura's speech-to-speech benchmark (11 models, 2,952 calls), GPT Realtime 2.1 alone reached 79.3% pass^3, while its mini sibling scored 50.0% and saved all fields correctly on only 64.5% of data-saving calls versus 92.0%.[42]

RECOVERY IS ITS OWN AXIS

A 2026 interruption benchmark found task completion and recovery quality only partly related.[43] Test barge-in and correction separately from happy paths.

RELEASE SCORECARD
Perception: word error rate on phone-grade audio, by accent and language, and on noisy, multi-speaker recordings (TV, car, a family member talking); entity accuracy for names, dates, phone and account numbers.
Routing: intent accuracy, out-of-scope recall, misroute rate (transfers after routing), calibration error.
Turn-taking: false and missed barge-ins, backchannel handling.
Latency: P95 turn time, not mean; dead air over 10 s.
Action correctness: exact argument match on every tool call. This is the hard gate.[23]
Repeatability: pass^3 on a golden set of 200+ scenarios; every production failure becomes a regression case; block a release if task completion drops more than 3 points or latency rises more than 10%.[27]
Emotion: frustration and confusion caught in real time on labeled calls, checked across accents and age groups; sentiment scored on every call after it ends.
Persona: each persona passes the same golden set; tone may differ, facts and policies may not.

WHEN A CLASSIFIER WINS, AND WHEN AN LLM WINS
Fine-tuned RoBERTa beat a small LLM on ATIS (95.9% vs 84.1%) and tied on CLINC150, at roughly three orders of magnitude lower cost and latency.[56]

THE THINKER-RESPONDER THESIS, CHECKED
HOLDS
Routing a call to a sub-agent or toolset is closed-set classification. Small decision models do it in roughly 20 to 250 ms for fractions of a cent, versus about 2 s for a frontier LLM.[8]
HOLDS
Narrowing tools first helps. Retrieving the right tools before selection lifted selection accuracy from 13.6% to 43.1% and cut prompt tokens by more than half in one study; Anthropic reports similar gains once agents have 10 or more tools.[58][59]
QUALIFY
For a fixed intent list, a plain fine-tuned classifier is often both the fastest and the most accurate router. Hosted decision APIs earn their place when intents change often or labeled data is scarce.[52][56]
QUALIFY
Decision models are not uniformly "great at decisions." Published accuracy for the same model on the same benchmark varies by more than 10 points across labs, and benign-looking context flipped about 61% of correct decisions in one study.[55][48]
GUARDRAIL
Fraud, clinical concern, complaints and vulnerable-customer signals escalate by rule, never by a threshold. Multi-intent calls ("overcharged again and nobody calls back") need multi-label output.[47]
ROUTING TO TOOLS AND MCP SERVERS
MCP-Zero selected from about 2,800 tools across 308 servers with 98% fewer tokens on one benchmark.[60]
Anthropic reports 58 tools across 5 servers consume about 55K tokens before a conversation starts; tool search lifted accuracy from 49% to 74% on one model.[59]
With 4,027 tools, the right tool reached the top results only 56% to 64% of the time, so retrieval recall becomes the new bottleneck.[61]
RECOMMENDED ROUTER DESIGN
Tier 0: fine-tuned encoder or self-hosted decision model, multi-label (per-intent sigmoid), so "billing and complaint" returns both. In bilingual markets, start from a multilingual encoder such as XLM-R and test turns that mix languages; English-only encoders misroute them.
Stop control: when the thinker flags risk while the agent is already speaking, it can flush and mute the audio output immediately, then hand off.
Calibrate with temperature scaling on your own data; set per-intent thresholds on a held-out set, never the test set.
Gate: below threshold, out of scope, or two high-stakes intents goes to a frontier model with the full schema, or to a person.
Hard rules: fraud, clinical concern, complaints and vulnerable-customer signals always escalate.
Narrow tools: each sub-agent exposes only its own MCP servers, with a broader-tools fallback when retrieval scores are low.
Injection hygiene: classify the caller's turn only, without retrieved or third-party text in context.[48]
Who owns state: one call-state object per call, held by the orchestrator. The thinker runs on every finished turn and writes typed fields (intent, risk, speaker, emotion, confidence). The responder owns the conversation, slot filling and clarifying questions ("does Dr. Smith work Fridays?"), and reads the thinker's fields before it speaks. A new intent, topic shift or correction triggers a re-route; slots already filled carry over.
Audit: log input hash, label, probabilities, threshold, model version and final disposition; re-run evals on every model change.
Compliance: hosted decision APIs need a BAA or equivalent for protected data; self-hosted encoders avoid the question.

T6 · LIMITED MEMORY LANGUAGE MODELS

How LMLM works, and what it has shown

Zhao et al., "Pre-training Limited Memory Language Models with Internal and External Knowledge," ICLR 2026. Code and 176M and 382M checkpoints are public under MIT.[11][35]

MECHANISM
Training text is annotated with inline lookups: entity, relation, then the retrieved value.
Loss is zeroed on the retrieved value tokens. The model still trains on every other token, so it learns when and how to look up, not the value itself.
The store holds 54.6M (entity, relation, value) triples extracted from Wikipedia by an 8B annotator trained on GPT-4o-labeled seeds.
At inference, a lookup token pauses generation; the query is matched by embedding similarity (rejection threshold 0.6) and the value is inserted.
Budget: about 3B tokens, 8 epochs, at most 8 H100-days per model.
FOLLOW-UP: CO-LMLM (JULY 2026)
Replaces relational triples with vector keys and free-text values, so knowledge is no longer limited to entity attributes.
About 2.2 ms per retrieval versus 28.6 ms for a question-generating lookup, which fits inside a voice turn.
Reports that lookup timing is imperfect: forcing lookups added 5.9 points on SimpleQA.[17]

WHAT HAS TO BE TRUE FOR REGULATED CONTACT CENTERS
Extraction quality: turning CRM, EHR and policy text into rows relies on LLM annotators; their errors become confident wrong answers.
Beyond entity facts: contact center knowledge is often procedural ("if the plan is X and the claim is Y"). Free-text values help, unproven here.
Reliable lookups: the model must know when to look up and must not fall back on weights.
Live data still needs tools: balances and appointments change by the minute; LMLM covers static knowledge, not transactions.
Total cost versus RAG: pre-training a private small model plus building and refreshing the store must beat retrieval on a frontier API.
Evidence at scale: nothing yet on dialog, speech, models above about 400M, or enterprise data. A 2026 position paper argues for this direction but reports no deployments.[66]

T11 · TOOLING AND IMPLEMENTATION

What to borrow, what to avoid
FRONTEND AND DEVELOPER TOOLING

Vercel's AI Voice Elements give React components for persona, mic capture, transcript and playback; useful for web prototypes, irrelevant to telephony.[84]

WHY IMPLEMENTATIONS FAIL

Abstract goals instead of concrete use cases, bots isolated from scheduling and CRM systems, no handoff path, messy knowledge, and no measurement after launch.[85] vendor-reported OpenAI's own rebuild relied on a silent production test to find failures synthetic load tests missed.[37][86]
