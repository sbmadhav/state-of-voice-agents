STATE OF CONVERSATIONAL VOICE AGENTS · HEALTHCARE FIRST, WITH NOTES FOR FINANCIAL SERVICES

November 2026 edition

Voice agents at the front door

Voice agents are now good enough to take routine calls in regulated contact centers: scheduling, billing questions, balance and status checks. They are not yet consistent enough to run unsupervised on clinical or high-risk financial conversations. In one vendor-run benchmark, the leading platform still failed about one in eight test scenarios when each was run three times, and in another, a TV playing in the room broke most agents.#

THE ONE-MINUTE READ

What we know. The best platform passed 87.8% of test scenarios on all three tries, and its lead over the runner-up is within the margin of error. Background speech is the failure most teams miss: with a TV on, task completion fell from 39% to 9% across 14 systems in one test. Every published deployment result is administrative and self-reported.

What to watch. Demos overstate readiness, cloned voices defeat voice-only identity checks, and scores move so fast that any model bet goes stale in a quarter.

What to do. Build a golden test set from your own calls and gate every change on it. Pilot narrow, judged on cost per resolved call. Separate silent decisions from the conversation, and build a governed fact store; trial limited-memory models only once they beat retrieval.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY · ABOUT TEN MINUTES

What this report argues, in plain terms

Voice agents can now take routine calls in regulated contact centers. What separates the teams that succeed is not which model they buy, but how they test, how the agent decides, what it remembers, who it is listening to, and how it proves who the caller is. Nine points, each linked to the evidence.

1
Ready for routine calls, not for unsupervised high-risk ones

Agents now handle scheduling, billing questions and status checks well. In one public benchmark, the best platform completed 98.8% of scenarios on a single try, but passed 87.8% when each scenario had to succeed three times in a row; the runner-up fell from 93.9% to 75.6%.[2] The gap is consistency, not capability. Health systems report large gains, such as waits cut from 6.2 to 2.4 minutes and 44% of repetitive calls automated, but every published result is administrative and self-reported.[5][6]

Reliability and results
2
Evaluation is the asset that lasts

Models change every month; the top score on one voice benchmark rose from 30% to 67% in eight months.[7] What you keep is a golden test set: hundreds of real, de-identified call scenarios with known right answers, including noisy lines and upset callers, run three times on every change. A drop in score blocks the release, the way failing tests block code. Build it before choosing a vendor: it is how you compare vendors, gate releases and hold contracts to account.

The golden test set
3
Think silently, then respond

People size up a question before they answer it. A voice agent should too. A small, fast "System 1" model answers typed questions in tens of milliseconds, each with a confidence score: which team owns this, is there fraud or clinical risk, is the caller finished, is it even the caller speaking. It never writes prose. A conversational LLM then responds, with the right data already fetched.

This is faster and cheaper: in one test, routing first kept accuracy at about 84% while cutting cost from $2.42 to about $0.69 per 1,000 requests.[8] It is also safer, because every decision is a logged record, and fraud, clinical and complaint signals always go to a person by rule. It works beside any voice stack, and it can cut the audio mid-sentence if it spots risk.

The thinker-responder model
4
Hear the right caller

Real calls come from cars, kitchens and waiting rooms. In one vendor test, background speech pushed transcription errors to 22% to 31%, and isolating the main speaker cut them by up to 43%.[9] In another, a TV playing in the room cut task completion across 14 voice systems from 38.7% to 8.6%, and nine of them fell below 5%.[10] Diarization separates the voices, so the agent locks onto the caller and checks identity again if a different voice takes over before a sensitive step. Test with noise and side conversations, not clean demo audio.

Noise and diarization
5
Emotion and persona: worth piloting, not yet proven

Detecting frustration in real time lets the agent slow down or hand off to a person, and scoring sentiment on every call replaces quality reviews that sample a few percent. A persona is the brand's voice: offer callers a choice, then keep each caller's preferred voice consistent. The evidence is still mostly vendor case studies, and the EU requires telling people when emotion recognition is used,[11] so run both as experiments behind the golden test set.

Emotion and persona
6
Build governed memory now; train limited-memory models later

Turn plans, policies, fees and provider details into a governed fact store: versioned rows with owners, access control and deletion. It powers retrieval today. Limited-memory models go further: they are trained to look facts up in that store instead of memorizing them. In research, a 382M-parameter model approached a 7B model on factual precision, and deleting a row made the model forget the fact.[12] That research is early (Wikipedia facts, small models, no voice or production use), so trial it on one domain once the store is ready, and scale only if it beats retrieval. Live data such as balances and appointment slots always comes from your systems through tools.

Governed memory
7
A voice is no longer proof of identity

Over half of fraud attempts at healthcare contact centers now involve AI-generated elements such as synthetic voice.[4] Match the check to the risk: passive signals for status checks, a one-time code for account changes, voiceprint plus liveness detection for irreversible actions, and always a person as the fallback. Card numbers should never reach the agent at all. For outbound calls, consent belongs to the current subscriber, so check lists against the FCC's Reassigned Numbers Database.[13]

Trust and identity
8
The money is in resolution, not token price

Savings come from calls the agent truly resolves, meaning no repeat contact on any channel within 48 hours. The calls left for people are the harder ones, so honest models show smaller savings than vendor pitches. With this report's default assumptions, the agent breaks even at about 40% resolution, and each extra point of resolution is worth far more than cheaper tokens. Contract on price per resolved call, with rights to export logs and evaluation data.

The economics model
9
What to do in the next 90 days

Pick two or three inbound administrative call types in one market. Build the golden test set (200+ scenarios). Set the go-live bar by risk: about 85% repeat reliability for informational calls, 95% for anything that writes data or moves money, and zero critical failures. Transfer to a person with full context. At day 90, judge on cost per resolved call, abandonment and zero wrong-account actions. Whatever the outcome, keep the test set, routing logs and recordings: they are the most valuable thing the pilot produces.

5 · THINK SILENTLY, THEN RESPOND

A thinker-responder model for conversational agents

People don't say every thought out loud before they answer. We size up the question, decide what it's about, recall what we need, and then speak. A voice agent should work the same way: a fast System-1 model is the thinker, making typed decisions silently, and a conversational LLM is the responder, turning those decisions into a natural reply.[26][24][23]

WHAT THE THINKER DECIDES (SYSTEM 1, SILENT, TYPED)
Intent: which sub-agent owns this turn (billing, scheduling, complaint)
Risk: fraud, clinical, vulnerable-caller or complaint flags that force a handoff
Tools: which MCP servers to expose, and which facts to fetch before speaking
Turn state: is the caller done, correcting themselves, or just saying "mm-hmm"
Speaker: is this the caller, or someone else in the room
Emotion: frustration or confusion that should change the tone, slow things down or hand off
Confidence: a probability on each, so low-confidence turns hand off instead of guessing
WHY IT WORKS
Faster: decisions take tens to hundreds of milliseconds, so the responder starts with data already fetched.
Cheaper: the expensive model only writes the reply, not the routing.
Safer: the "thought" is a structured record, not prose, so it can be logged, audited and gated by rules. The responder can't narrate a tool result it never received.[27]
A deliberate difference: OpenAI and Sierra put the fast model on the talking side and a slower reasoner behind it.[39][18] This design puts the fast model on the deciding side. The two combine: a fast talker, a fast silent decider, and a slower reasoner when needed.
Works with any voice stack: the thinker does not need a speech-to-text pipeline. Beside a speech-to-speech or full-duplex model it reads the transcript stream and decides which tools and policies apply, so the voice model keeps its natural turn-taking. Because a full-duplex model starts speaking within a few hundred milliseconds, the thinker also needs a stop control: when it flags risk mid-turn, it cuts the audio output at once, before protected data or advice reaches the caller.
Limit: the thinker handles bounded choices. Open-ended reasoning still needs a deeper planner the responder can call.

6 · WHAT SEPARATES A GOOD AGENT

Two foundations, and two experiments worth running

Models change every month. Two capabilities decide whether an agent holds up on real calls, and they carry over from one model or vendor to the next. Two more are promising but early: pilot them behind the golden test set.

FOUNDATION · A GOLDEN TEST SET AND AN EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

The most valuable asset a team builds. Hundreds of real, de-identified call scenarios, each with a known right answer, run three times on every change to models, prompts, tools or personas.

Common guidance: 200+ scenarios before production, 500+ for multilingual enterprise use, [40] vendor-reported To detect a 5-point change in a pass rate near 80%, plan for several hundred runs per version, not the 100 sometimes quoted. author's arithmetic
Mix happy paths with edge cases, errors, adversarial callers and noisy audio, and add every production failure as a new case.
Block a release when a score drops, the same way failing tests block code.
FOUNDATION · HEARING THE RIGHT CALLER

Real callers phone from cars, kitchens and waiting rooms, with a TV on or someone talking beside them. How the agent performs there matters more than how it performs in a quiet demo.

Background speech pushed streaming speech-to-text error rates to 22% to 31%; isolating the main speaker cut errors by up to 43%.[9] vendor-reported
A TV is the worst case. In a vendor test of 14 voice systems on simulated calls, task completion fell from 38.7% on clean calls to 8.6% with a TV playing, and caller verification fell from 81% to 53%.[10] vendor research
Open tools now exist for this: VoxLock (Apache-2.0, 27M parameters) learns the caller's voice in the first seconds and removes the rest, cutting transcription errors from 23% to 5% in its author's tests.[36] author-reported
Separate the voices (diarization), lock onto the caller, and re-verify identity if a different voice takes over before a sensitive step.[41]
Test at several noise levels and with side conversations, not just clean audio.
EXPERIMENT · EMOTION IN REAL TIME, SENTIMENT ON EVERY CALL

During the call, frustration or confusion should change how the agent speaks or trigger a warm handoff. After the call, sentiment scored on every call replaces quality reviews that sample a few percent.

Treat emotion scores as a signal for routing and review, not a judgment of the caller. Accuracy varies, so check that they work equally across accents and age groups.
In the EU, people must be told when an emotion recognition system is used.[11]
EXPERIMENT · PERSONA IS THE BRAND

Callers judge the organization by how its agent sounds. Voice, pace, warmth and word choice deserve the same care as a brand guide.

One subscription business reported resolution rates up more than 50% after tuning its voice persona.[18] vendor-reported
Offer callers a choice of a few personas, then keep each caller's preferred voice consistent on every call. author's view
Every persona says it is an AI, never implies a professional license, and gives the same facts and policies.[42]

author's view The experiments have little evidence so far: mostly vendor case studies, and none yet shows emotion detection or personas improving AI-agent outcomes at scale.

A voice is no longer proof of identity

This is a brand question as much as a security one. Customers will be targeted by callers pretending to be you, and your agent will hear callers pretending to be customers.[43]

Liveness and deepfake detection in the contact center platform, before any protected data.
Step-up verification (one-time code to the number on file), not birth date or security questions alone.
For payments, prescription changes and other authorizations, add voiceprint verification with liveness detection; a voiceprint alone can be fooled by a clone.[44]
Match the friction to the risk: passive signals for status checks, a one-time code for account changes, voiceprint plus liveness only for irreversible actions. Liveness checks over phone-quality audio reject some genuine callers, and texted codes lose some older or distressed callers, so always offer a person as the fallback. author's view
A published "we will never ask you for" policy, owned by marketing.
In the US, outbound AI-voice calls count as artificial-voice calls under the TCPA since the FCC's 2024 ruling, so they generally need prior express consent; healthcare exemptions are narrow, so check reminder programs with counsel.[45] Consent belongs to the current subscriber, so check outbound lists against the FCC Reassigned Numbers Database before every campaign.[13]

9 · WHAT COMES NEXT

Models that look facts up instead of memorizing them

A Limited Memory Language Model (LMLM) is trained so that specific facts, such as a plan's coverage or a card's limit, live in an external database, not in the model's weights. During training the model is not rewarded for memorizing those values; it learns to issue a lookup and copy the answer. Language and reasoning stay in the model. Facts stay in a table people can inspect, correct and delete.[12] It suits slow-changing knowledge such as benefits, policies and fees; live data such as balances, claims and appointment slots stays on tool and MCP calls.

illustrative The deletion behavior mirrors the paper's unlearning result: removing records from the store made the model forget them with no loss of general ability, where gradient-based unlearning degraded the model.[12] Logs, transcripts, caches and backups still need their own deletion.

WHY A GOVERNED FACT STORE FITS REGULATED CONVERSATIONS
Facts become governed data. Each one is a row with access control, versioning and change review.
Deletion from the model is real. Removing rows forgets them without retraining.[12] Copies in logs, transcripts and backups need their own retention rules.
Less sensitive data in weights. Researchers have extracted names, phone numbers and emails from model weights, even from data seen once.[46][47]
Every answer has provenance. Each lookup is a logged call with the value it returned.
Room to shrink the model. In research, a 382M limited-memory model came close to a 7B model on factual precision; details are in the technical appendix (T6).[12]
MY VIEW: BUILD THE FACT STORE NOW, AND TREAT LIMITED-MEMORY TRAINING AS A GATED BET

Retrieval on a frontier model is the safe default today, and the evidence for limited-memory models is still research-stage: Wikipedia facts, models under 400M parameters, no dialog, voice or production use. A governed fact store pays off either way. Training is a bet to stage behind gates, not a commitment.

Now · no-regret
Build the governed fact store

Turn plans, policies, products, providers and fees into versioned rows with owners, access control and deletion. It powers retrieval today and LMLM tomorrow.

When the store is ready · trial
Trial a small model on one domain

Start from the open LMLM code (MIT) on one de-identified domain, such as benefits or product FAQs. Go further only if it beats retrieval on factual precision, deletion, latency and total cost.[48]

Only if it wins · scale
Put it behind the responder

Expand domains for static knowledge. Live balances, claims and appointment slots always stay on tools.

Compute is not the hard part: the paper trained each model in at most 8 H100-days[12], roughly $400 to $500 at $2 to $2.69 an hour for rented H100s[49]. author's arithmetic Budget for data engineering, store quality and evaluation instead.

11 · A PILOT PLAYBOOK

A 90-day pilot with gates set before launch

Teams are at different points on this path. If you are starting out, expanding to new call types or resetting a stalled rollout, this is one way to run it; if you are already in production, the golden test set, gates and kill criteria work as an audit of what you have. The shape: one market or line of business, inbound only, two or three call types, warm transfer with full context. The pre-launch bar is set above the best public benchmark on purpose, because public scenarios are generic.

Weeks 0 to 4
Build the golden test set

200+ scenarios from your own de-identified calls: accents, older callers, Spanish, number dictation, mid-call corrections, background noise and chatter, upset callers. Silent shadow test on recorded audio.

Gate · end of week 4
Pass^3 bar set by risk

About 85% for informational calls, 95% for anything that writes data or moves money, and zero critical failures at any rate. Every scenario run three times on phone-grade audio. Router thresholds calibrated on held-out data. Identity controls signed off.

Weeks 5 to 12
Live in one market

Every failure becomes a regression test. Weekly audit of tool-call write-backs and misroutes (transfers after routing).

Day 90
Scale, adjust, or stop

Decide against the criteria below, not against a demo.

author's view Starting points tied to the published results above, not an industry standard. The pre-launch bar is a risk-management choice, not a benchmark-derived number: a narrow golden set of two or three call types is easier than a broad generic benchmark, so it can sit above the best public score (87.8%), and the bar rises with the harm a failure can do. A high overall pass rate never offsets a critical failure, such as protected data given to the wrong caller or a wrong payment; one of those blocks launch. With 200 scenarios, a measured 90% carries a margin of about plus or minus 4 points. 30% lower abandonment is about half what peers report (56% to 85%). 50% resolution is in the range of reported automation (44% at one health system). Tighten the bar for higher-risk call types, and lower the resolution target for complex queues.

T2 · LATENCY BUDGET

Where the seconds go in a turn

Human turn gaps across ten languages cluster around a quarter second.[57] Measured platform turns in the benchmark averaged 1.27 s to 3.08 s.[2] Adjust the stages, or pick a preset.

Be candid about the trade-off: a cascade (speech-to-text, LLM, text-to-speech) takes turns like a walkie-talkie. RECOVERY IS ITS OWN AXIS

A 2026 interruption benchmark found task completion and recovery quality only partly related.[61] Test barge-in and correction separately from happy paths.

RELEASE SCORECARD
Perception: word error rate on phone-grade audio, by accent and language, and on noisy, multi-speaker recordings (TV, car, a family member talking); entity accuracy for names, dates, phone and account numbers.
Routing: intent accuracy, out-of-scope recall, misroute rate (transfers after routing), calibration error.
Turn-taking: false and missed barge-ins, backchannel handling.
Latency: P95 turn time, not mean; dead air over 10 s.
Action correctness: exact argument match on every tool call. This is the hard gate.[27]
Repeatability: pass^3 on a golden set of 200+ scenarios; every production failure becomes a regression case; block a release if task completion drops more than 3 points or latency rises more than 10%.[40]
Emotion: frustration and confusion caught in real time on labeled calls, checked across accents and age groups; sentiment scored on every call after it ends.
Persona: each persona passes the same golden set; tone may differ, facts and policies may not.

HIGHLIGHT · THE TV IN THE ROOM

The noise that hurts most is not traffic or wind. It is another voice. Veris called 14 voice systems as a simulated utility-billing customer, 100 tasks run three times each, with street, café and TV audio mixed in at equal loudness. With the TV on, task completion fell from 38.7% (372 of 961 calls) to 8.6% (81 of 945). Nine of the 14 systems fell below 5%; only GPT-Live 1 (36.1%) and Grok Voice (33.8%) stayed above 20%. Caller verification fell from 81.3% to 52.5%, and 90% of TV calls carried 10 or more words the caller never said, against 2% of clean calls.[10] vendor research Veris tested with each vendor's noise options left off, and the calls were simulated, so treat the levels as directional. The direction is not in doubt.

For a regulated contact center, those inserted words are the risk: a newsreader's number in a payment amount, a bystander answering a security question, the agent stopping mid-disclosure because the TV spoke. Generic noise suppression does not remove speech. Caller-voice isolation does.

WHEN A CLASSIFIER WINS, AND WHEN AN LLM WINS
Fine-tuned RoBERTa beat a small LLM on ATIS (95.9% vs 84.1%) and tied on CLINC150, at roughly three orders of magnitude lower cost and latency.[78]
The LLM won on out-of-scope recall (85.6% vs 58.1%) and on very noisy speech (92.5% vs 80.0%).[78]
A classifier trained on one app's intents scored 0% on a new app's; a schema-prompted model served both at about 94%.[78]
Implication: classifier for stable, high-volume intents; decision API or LLM for new, rare or out-of-scope ones.

THE THINKER-RESPONDER THESIS, CHECKED
HOLDS
Routing a call to a sub-agent or toolset is closed-set classification. Small decision models do it in roughly 20 to 250 ms for fractions of a cent, versus about 2 s for a frontier LLM.[8]
HOLDS
Narrowing tools first helps. Retrieving the right tools before selection lifted selection accuracy from 13.6% to 43.1% and cut prompt tokens by more than half in one study; Anthropic reports similar gains once agents have 10 or more tools.[82][83]
QUALIFY
For a fixed intent list, a plain fine-tuned classifier is often both the fastest and the most accurate router. Hosted decision APIs earn their place when intents change often or labeled data is scarce.[74][78]
QUALIFY
Decision models are not uniformly "great at decisions." Published accuracy for the same model on the same benchmark varies by more than 10 points across labs, and benign-looking context flipped about 61% of correct decisions in one study.[77][66]
GUARDRAIL
Fraud, clinical concern, complaints and vulnerable-customer signals escalate by rule, never by a threshold. Multi-intent calls ("overcharged again and nobody calls back") need multi-label output.[65]
ROUTING TO TOOLS AND MCP SERVERS
MCP-Zero selected from about 2,800 tools across 308 servers with 98% fewer tokens on one benchmark.[84]
Anthropic reports 58 tools across 5 servers consume about 55K tokens before a conversation starts; tool search lifted accuracy from 49% to 74% on one model.[83]
With 4,027 tools, the right tool reached the top results only 56% to 64% of the time, so retrieval recall becomes the new bottleneck.[85]
RECOMMENDED ROUTER DESIGN
Tier 0: fine-tuned encoder or self-hosted decision model, multi-label (per-intent sigmoid), so "billing and complaint" returns both. In bilingual markets, start from a multilingual encoder such as XLM-R and test turns that mix languages; English-only encoders misroute them.
Stop control: when the thinker flags risk while the agent is already speaking, it can flush and mute the audio output immediately, then hand off.
Calibrate with temperature scaling on your own data; set per-intent thresholds on a held-out set, never the test set.
Gate: below threshold, out of scope, or two high-stakes intents goes to a frontier model with the full schema, or to a person.
Hard rules: fraud, clinical concern, complaints and vulnerable-customer signals always escalate.
Narrow tools: each sub-agent exposes only its own MCP servers, with a broader-tools fallback when retrieval scores are low.
Injection hygiene: classify the caller's turn only, without retrieved or third-party text in context.[66]
Who owns state: one call-state object per call, held by the orchestrator. The thinker runs on every finished turn and writes typed fields (intent, risk, speaker, emotion, confidence). The responder owns the conversation, slot filling and clarifying questions ("does Dr. Smith work Fridays?"), and reads the thinker's fields before it speaks. A new intent, topic shift or correction triggers a re-route; slots already filled carry over.
Audit: log input hash, label, probabilities, threshold, model version and final disposition; re-run evals on every model change.
Compliance: hosted decision APIs need a BAA or equivalent for protected data; self-hosted encoders avoid the question.

T6 · LIMITED MEMORY LANGUAGE MODELS

How LMLM works, and what it has shown

Zhao et al., "Pre-training Limited Memory Language Models with Internal and External Knowledge," ICLR 2026. Code and 176M and 382M checkpoints are public under MIT.[12][48]

MECHANISM
Training text is annotated with inline lookups: entity, relation, then the retrieved value.
Loss is zeroed on the retrieved value tokens. The model still trains on every other token, so it learns when and how to look up, not the value itself.
The store holds 54.6M (entity, relation, value) triples extracted from Wikipedia by an 8B annotator trained on GPT-4o-labeled seeds.
At inference, a lookup token pauses generation; the query is matched by embedding similarity (rejection threshold 0.6) and the value is inserted.
Budget: about 3B tokens, 8 epochs, at most 8 H100-days per model.
FOLLOW-UP: CO-LMLM (JULY 2026)
Replaces relational triples with vector keys and free-text values, so knowledge is no longer limited to entity attributes.
About 2.2 ms per retrieval versus 28.6 ms for a question-generating lookup, which fits inside a voice turn.
Reports that lookup timing is imperfect: forcing lookups added 5.9 points on SimpleQA.[21]

Parameter reduction at comparable performance on knowledge-heavy tasks.[12][86][87] Models store about 2 bits of knowledge per parameter, falling toward 1 bit for rarely seen facts.[88] Question-answering accuracy tracks how often a fact appears in training data.[89] None of these measure the share of a frontier model's weights spent on facts; reasoning, code and multilingual skill need parameters too.

CLAIM OFTEN MADE	VERDICT	DEFENSIBLE VERSION
Every fact it shares is correct; it cannot hallucinate	OVERSTATED	It cuts hallucination sharply and makes each fact traceable; errors from wrong lookups, missing rows and extraction remain.
It keeps non-repeating data out of the model	PARTLY	It offloads factual attributes by type, common and rare alike; rare facts are where models learn worst.
Fact storage is ~99% of model bloat	UNSUPPORTED	Retrieval models match models 18 to 50 times larger on knowledge tasks; the exact share is unmeasured.
It makes training cheap	PARTLY	It still trains on the full corpus and adds lookup and store-building cost; the saving is fewer parameters for a given factual accuracy, which lowers serving cost.
It keeps sensitive data out of weights	PLAUSIBLE, UNTESTED	Designed to, and extraction attacks show why it matters, but no privacy-attack evaluation has been published.[46][47]
WHAT HAS TO BE TRUE FOR REGULATED CONTACT CENTERS
Extraction quality: turning CRM, EHR and policy text into rows relies on LLM annotators; their errors become confident wrong answers.
Beyond entity facts: contact center knowledge is often procedural ("if the plan is X and the claim is Y"). Free-text values help, unproven here.
Reliable lookups: the model must know when to look up and must not fall back on weights.
Live data still needs tools: balances and appointments change by the minute; LMLM covers static knowledge, not transactions.
Total cost versus RAG: pre-training a private small model plus building and refreshing the store must beat retrieval on a frontier API.
Evidence at scale: nothing yet on dialog, speech, models above about 400M, or enterprise data. A 2026 position paper argues for this direction but reports no deployments.[90]

T7 · DEPLOYMENT AND DATA POSTURE

"Sovereign" in practice means private endpoints under contract

The case for keeping protected data and conversation logs under your control is strong. The case for running live conversational voice on your own GPUs is weak for 2026 to 2027. The exceptions are growing: small routers, speech output, and transcription of recorded calls now have credible open models.

OPEN VERSUS CLOSED MODELS

Enterprise surveys put open-source models near 11% of LLM API share.[98][99] Open models win on cheap, high-volume work, which is exactly the router's job.
