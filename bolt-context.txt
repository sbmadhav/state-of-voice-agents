STATE OF CONVERSATIONAL VOICE AGENTS · HEALTHCARE AND FINANCIAL SERVICES

October 2026 edition

Voice agents at the front door

Voice agents are now good enough to take routine calls in regulated contact centers: scheduling, billing questions, balance and status checks. They are not yet consistent enough to run unsupervised on clinical or high-risk financial conversations. The leading platform still fails about one in four test scenarios when each one is run three times.#

A SUGGESTED PATH

Invest in evaluation first. Build a golden test set from your own calls, including noisy lines, side conversations and upset callers, and gate every change on it.

Pilot narrow and measure repeatability. 90 days, inbound only, two or three call types, warm transfer to a human, judged on cost per resolved call.

Think silently, then respond. A fast System-1 model does the "thinking" (intent, risk, which sub-agent and tools) without saying a word; a conversational LLM does the responding.

After day 90, scale what passed. Add call types and markets in waves through the same gate, bring routing and evaluation in-house, and move contracts to price per resolved call.

Invest in limited-memory model training now. Build a governed fact store from your systems of record and train a small model on one knowledge domain, run side by side with retrieval.

5 · THINK SILENTLY, THEN RESPOND

A thinker-responder model for conversational agents

People don't say every thought out loud before they answer. We size up the question, decide what it's about, recall what we need, and then speak. A voice agent should work the same way: a fast System-1 model is the thinker, making typed decisions silently, and a conversational LLM is the responder, turning those decisions into a natural reply.[20][18][17]

ONE CALLER TURN, TWO WAYS
Caller: "I got charged twice for my visit last month."

risk = none
tools = billing-mcp
fetch = recent charges
~50 ms
RESPONDER · SPEAKS
"I can see two charges of $140 on March 3. I'll reverse the duplicate now. Is that the card ending 4471?"

The caller hears one direct answer. The decisions are logged as structured data, not spoken.

WHAT THE THINKER DECIDES (SYSTEM 1, SILENT, TYPED)
Intent: which sub-agent owns this turn (billing, scheduling, complaint)
Risk: fraud, clinical, vulnerable-caller or complaint flags that force a handoff
Tools: which MCP servers to expose, and which facts to fetch before speaking
Turn state: is the caller done, correcting themselves, or just saying "mm-hmm"
Speaker: is this the caller, or someone else in the room
Emotion: frustration or confusion that should change the tone, slow things down or hand off
Confidence: a probability on each, so low-confidence turns hand off instead of guessing
WHY IT WORKS
Faster: decisions take tens to hundreds of milliseconds, so the responder starts with data already fetched.
Cheaper: the expensive model only writes the reply, not the routing.
Safer: the "thought" is a structured record, not prose, so it can be logged, audited and gated by rules. The responder can't narrate a tool result it never received.[21]
Converging with the industry: OpenAI and Sierra also split talking from thinking, though both put the fast model on the talking side and a slower reasoner behind it.[24][11]
Limit: the thinker handles bounded choices. Open-ended reasoning still needs a deeper planner the responder can call.

WHAT A CASCADE BUYS

In one public test on 3,080 banking intents, a frontier model scored 84.4% at about $2.42 per 1,000 requests. Sending everything to a decision model first, and only the low-confidence cases (below 0.90) on to the frontier model, scored 84.0% at about $0.69 per 1,000.[4]

THE THESIS, CHECKED
holds
Routing a call to a sub-agent or toolset is closed-set classification. Small decision models do it in roughly 20 to 250 ms for fractions of a cent, versus about 2 s for a frontier LLM.[4]
holds
Narrowing tools first helps. Retrieving the right tools before selection lifted selection accuracy from 13.6% to 43.1% and cut prompt tokens by more than half in one study; Anthropic reports similar gains once agents have 10 or more tools.[27][28]
qualify
For a fixed intent list, a plain fine-tuned classifier is often both the fastest and the most accurate router. Hosted decision APIs earn their place when intents change often or labeled data is scarce.[3][29]
qualify
Decision models are not uniformly "great at decisions." Published accuracy for the same model on the same benchmark varies by more than 10 points across labs, and benign-looking context flipped about 61% of correct decisions in one study.[30][31]
guardrail
Fraud, clinical concern, complaints and vulnerable-customer signals escalate by rule, never by a threshold. Multi-intent calls ("overcharged again and nobody calls back") need multi-label output.[32]

6 · WHAT SEPARATES A GOOD AGENT

Four investments that decide whether callers trust it

Models change every month. These four capabilities are what make an agent hold up on real calls, and they carry over from one model or vendor to the next.

1 · A GOLDEN TEST SET AND AN EVALUATION FRAMEWORK

The most valuable asset a team builds. Hundreds of real, de-identified call scenarios, each with a known right answer, run three times on every change to models, prompts, tools or personas.

Common guidance: 200+ scenarios before production, 500+ for multilingual enterprise use, and about 100 observations to detect a 5% change with confidence.[33] vendor guidance
Mix happy paths with edge cases, errors, adversarial callers and noisy audio, and add every production failure as a new case.
Block a release when a score drops, the same way failing tests block code.
2 · HEARING THE RIGHT CALLER

Real callers phone from cars, kitchens and waiting rooms, with a TV on or someone talking beside them. How the agent performs there matters more than how it performs in a quiet demo.

Background speech pushed streaming speech-to-text error rates to 22% to 31%; isolating the main speaker cut errors by up to 43%.[34] vendor test
Separate the voices (diarization), lock onto the caller, and re-verify identity if a different voice takes over before a sensitive step.[35]
Test at several noise levels and with side conversations, not just clean audio.
3 · EMOTION IN REAL TIME, SENTIMENT ON EVERY CALL

During the call, frustration or confusion should change how the agent speaks or trigger a warm handoff. After the call, sentiment scored on every call replaces quality reviews that sample a few percent.

One contact center pilot moved from reviewing 3% of calls to emotion-aware coaching, and escalations fell from 14% to 6%.[36] vendor case study, human agents
Treat emotion scores as a signal for routing and review, not a judgment of the caller, and check that they work equally across accents and age groups.
In the EU, people must be told when an emotion recognition system is used.[37]
4 · PERSONA IS THE BRAND

Callers judge the organization by how its agent sounds. Voice, pace, warmth and word choice deserve the same care as a brand guide.

One subscription business reported resolution rates up more than 50% after tuning its voice persona.[11] vendor-reported
Offering a few personas keeps repeat callers from hearing the same voice every time; let callers keep the one they prefer. author's view
Every persona says it is an AI, never implies a professional license, and gives the same facts and policies.[38]

Over half

of fraud attempts at healthcare contact centers now involve AI-generated elements such as synthetic voice, bots and IVR reconnaissance.[13] vendor-reported

A voice is no longer proof of identity

This is a brand question as much as a security one. Customers will be targeted by callers pretending to be you, and your agent will hear callers pretending to be customers.[39]

Liveness and deepfake detection in the contact center platform, before any protected data.
Step-up verification (one-time code to the number on file), not birth date or security questions alone.
For payments, prescription changes and other authorizations, add voiceprint verification with liveness detection; a voiceprint alone can be fooled by a clone.[40]
A published "we will never ask you for" policy, owned by marketing.
In the US, outbound AI calls need prior consent under the FCC's 2024 ruling, reminders included.[41]

9 · WHAT COMES NEXT

Models that look facts up instead of memorizing them

A Limited Memory Language Model (LMLM) is trained so that specific facts, such as a plan's coverage or a card's limit, live in an external database, not in the model's weights. During training the model is not rewarded for memorizing those values; it learns to issue a lookup and copy the answer. Language and reasoning stay in the model. Facts stay in a table people can inspect, correct and delete.[5]

WHY IT FITS REGULATED CONVERSATIONS
Facts become governed data. Each one is a row with access control, versioning and change review.
Deletion is real. Removing rows forgets them without retraining.[5]
Less sensitive data in weights. Researchers have extracted names, phone numbers and emails from model weights, even from data seen once.[42][43]
Every answer has provenance. Each lookup is a logged call with the value it returned.
Small enough to run privately. Sub-1B models with lookups can sit inside the enterprise boundary.
MY RECOMMENDATION: INVEST IN LIMITED-MEMORY TRAINING NOW, IN STAGES

Retrieval on a frontier model is the safe default today. But a governed fact store pays off with or without LMLM, and a first training run is cheap enough that waiting costs more than trying.

Now · no-regret
Build the governed fact store

Turn plans, policies, products, providers and fees into versioned rows with owners, access control and deletion. It powers retrieval today and LMLM tomorrow.

Next quarter · prove it
Train a small model on one domain

Start from the open LMLM code (MIT) on one de-identified domain, such as benefits or product FAQs. Score it against retrieval on factual precision, deletion, latency and cost.[44]

Within 12 months · scale
Put it behind the responder

If it wins on precision and governance, expand domains and let the responder use it for static knowledge. Live balances and appointments stay on tools.

Compute is not the hard part: the paper trained each model in at most 8 H100-days[5], roughly $400 to $500 at $2 to $2.69 an hour for rented H100s[45]. author's arithmetic Budget for data engineering, store quality and evaluation instead.

THE THESIS, CHECKED
holds
Moving facts into a store the model must consult sharply improves factual precision at a given size and makes each fact traceable and deletable.[5]
qualify
It reduces hallucination; it does not eliminate it. Wrong lookups, missing rows, extraction errors and the model answering from memory instead of looking up all remain. The authors say it "does not guarantee perfect factuality."[5][15]
qualify
Facts are selected by type (entity attributes), not by whether they repeat. Frequency still matters: models learn rare facts poorly, and capacity per fact drops when facts are seen less often.[46][47]
qualify
No study measures what share of a frontier model's weights is fact storage. Retrieval-based models have matched models 18 to 50 times their size on knowledge-heavy tasks, which suggests the share is large.[48][49]
open
Research stage: tested on Wikipedia with models under 400M parameters. No dialog, voice, enterprise data or production deployment yet.[44]

11 · RECOMMENDED NEXT STEP

A 90-day pilot with gates set before launch

One market or line of business, inbound only, two or three call types, warm transfer with full context. The pre-launch bar is set above the best public benchmark on purpose, because public scenarios are generic.

Weeks 0 to 4
Build the golden test set

200+ scenarios from your own de-identified calls: accents, older callers, Spanish, number dictation, mid-call corrections, background noise and chatter, upset callers. Silent shadow test on recorded audio.

Gate · end of week 4
90%+ pass^3 to go live

Every scenario run three times on phone-grade audio. Router thresholds calibrated on held-out data. Identity controls signed off.

Weeks 5 to 12
Live in one market

Every failure becomes a regression test. Weekly audit of tool-call write-backs and misroutes (transfers after routing).

Day 90
Scale, adjust, or stop

12 · THINK TANK

Seven seats at the table

A structured debate on one question: what should a regulated enterprise do about voice agents in the next 12 months, and how much should it bet on fast routers and governed-memory models?

Chief Digital & CX Officer

Expand in phases. Voice is the front door, and it is now also a brand-trust channel.

Evidence: A large health system cut abandonment 56% and scheduled 21% more appointments in two weeks. Concern: One bad call becomes the story. Scammers posing as your brand erode trust in every legitimate call. Would change their mind: Pilot satisfaction falling below the human baseline.

CFO

Fund a pilot, not a platform. Pay for resolved calls.

Evidence: The higher-resolution stack is cheaper per 1,000 calls despite a higher per-minute rate. A decision-first cascade cut classification cost about 72% in one test. Concern: Vertical vendors rarely publish pricing. Early-access model prices may be subsidized. Would change their mind: Verified cost per resolved call below half the human cost at day 90.

CISO & Privacy Officer

Hosted APIs only under contract. Self-host the small models. Prefer facts in governed stores.

Evidence: Hosted decision APIs need a BAA or equivalent; self-hosted encoders avoid the question. Extraction attacks have pulled personal data from model weights. Concern: Voice cloning, prompt injection, retention terms that change. Would change their mind: A signed contract and SOC 2 Type II from every layer, testing vendor included.

Contact Center Operations

Start with scheduling, billing and status checks; warm transfer with full context.

Evidence: Intermountain: 44% of repetitive calls automated and 85% lower abandonment. Concern: Wrong write-backs, misroutes, authentication failures, staff morale. Would change their mind: Misroute or wrong-action rate above 0.5% on audit.

Clinical & Risk Safety Lead

Administrative only. No triage, no advice. Escalation by rule, not by threshold.

Evidence: No validated autonomous triage deployment found. Best models reach only about 53% on multi-intent benchmarks. Concern: Missed red flags, older and non-English callers, regulatory exposure. Would change their mind: An independent safety evaluation with prospective outcomes.

AI Platform Architect

Thinker-responder: tiered router in front, narrowed tools per sub-agent, and an LMLM prototype for static knowledge. On a managed platform such as Sierra, insist on exporting routing decisions and logs.

Evidence: A 0.1B fine-tuned classifier hits 93.5% on Banking77 in 22 ms. Tool retrieval more than tripled selection accuracy. A 382M LMLM approached a 7B model on factual precision. Concern: Lock-in at orchestration and integration layers; model churn every few weeks. Would change their mind: A vendor refusing to export logs or routing decisions.

Skeptic / red team

Do not trust vendor benchmarks or one-lab results. Demand your own repeat-reliability numbers.

Evidence: Jev's Banking77 score varies 10.7 points by lab. Benign context flipped 61% of correct decisions. LMLM is untested above 400M parameters or on dialog. Sierra publishes single-run voice scores, not repeat reliability. Concern: Every outcome figure here is customer- or vendor-reported, with no control group. Would change their mind: 90%+ pass^3 on your own scenarios, audited by an independent party.

WHERE ALL SEVEN AGREE
Start with inbound administrative calls and warm human transfer.
Build a golden evaluation set before choosing a vendor, and gate every change on it.
Measure repeat reliability and cost per resolved call, not containment or demo quality.
Use a fast router, with calibrated thresholds and rule-based escalation.
Own the evaluation data, routing logs and call logs, whichever vendor wins.
STILL UNRESOLVED
Fine-tuned classifier (cheapest, most accurate on fixed intents) versus a hosted decision API (no training data, adapts to new intents).
When a private limited-memory model beats retrieval on a frontier model, on total cost.
Speed versus safety on outbound calls, where consent rules bite at scale.

At 94.0% per call, only 83.1% of scenarios would pass three for three, assuming independent runs. Across 30,000 calls a month, that per-call rate still means about 1,800 failed calls.

SAME MODEL, DIFFERENT HARNESS

In Cekura's speech-to-speech benchmark (11 models, 2,952 calls), GPT Realtime 2.1 alone reached 79.3% pass^3, while its mini sibling scored 50.0% and saved all fields correctly on only 64.5% of data-saving calls versus 92.0%.[55]

RECOVERY IS ITS OWN AXIS

A 2026 interruption benchmark found task completion and recovery quality only partly related.[56] Test barge-in and correction separately from happy paths.

RELEASE SCORECARD
Perception: word error rate on phone-grade audio, by accent and language, and on noisy, multi-speaker recordings (TV, car, a family member talking); entity accuracy for names, dates, phone and account numbers.
Routing: intent accuracy, out-of-scope recall, misroute rate (transfers after routing), calibration error.
Turn-taking: false and missed barge-ins, backchannel handling.
Latency: P95 turn time, not mean; dead air over 10 s.
Action correctness: exact argument match on every tool call. This is the hard gate.[21]
Repeatability: pass^3 on a golden set of 200+ scenarios; every production failure becomes a regression case; block a release if task completion drops more than 3 points or latency rises more than 10%.[33]
Emotion: frustration and confusion caught in real time on labeled calls, checked across accents and age groups; sentiment scored on every call after it ends.
Persona: each persona passes the same golden set; tone may differ, facts and policies may not.

WHEN A CLASSIFIER WINS, AND WHEN AN LLM WINS
Fine-tuned RoBERTa beat a small LLM on ATIS (95.9% vs 84.1%) and tied on CLINC150, at roughly three orders of magnitude lower cost and latency.[29]
The LLM won on out-of-scope recall (85.6% vs 58.1%) and on very noisy speech (92.5% vs 80.0%).[29]
A classifier trained on one app's intents scored 0% on a new app's; a schema-prompted model served both at about 94%.[29]
Implication: classifier for stable, high-volume intents; decision API or LLM for new, rare or out-of-scope ones.
ROUTING TO TOOLS AND MCP SERVERS
Retrieving candidate tools before selection raised selection accuracy from 13.6% to 43.1% and cut prompt tokens by more than half.[27]
MCP-Zero selected from about 2,800 tools across 308 servers with 98% fewer tokens on one benchmark.[64]
Anthropic reports 58 tools across 5 servers consume about 55K tokens before a conversation starts; tool search lifted accuracy from 49% to 74% on one model.[28]
With 4,027 tools, the right tool reached the top results only 56% to 64% of the time, so retrieval recall becomes the new bottleneck.[65]
RECOMMENDED ROUTER DESIGN
Tier 0: fine-tuned encoder or self-hosted decision model, multi-label (per-intent sigmoid), so "billing and complaint" returns both.
Calibrate with temperature scaling on your own data; set per-intent thresholds on a held-out set, never the test set.
Gate: below threshold, out of scope, or two high-stakes intents goes to a frontier model with the full schema, or to a person.
Hard rules: fraud, clinical concern, complaints and vulnerable-customer signals always escalate.
Narrow tools: each sub-agent exposes only its own MCP servers, with a broader-tools fallback when retrieval scores are low.
Injection hygiene: classify the caller's turn only, without retrieved or third-party text in context.[31]
Audit: log input hash, label, probabilities, threshold, model version and final disposition; re-run evals on every model change.
Compliance: hosted decision APIs need a BAA or equivalent for protected data; self-hosted encoders avoid the question.

T6 · LIMITED MEMORY LANGUAGE MODELS

How LMLM works, and what it has shown

Zhao et al., "Pre-training Limited Memory Language Models with Internal and External Knowledge," ICLR 2026. Code and 176M and 382M checkpoints are public under MIT.[5][44]

MECHANISM
Training text is annotated with inline lookups: entity, relation, then the retrieved value.
Loss is zeroed on the retrieved value tokens. The model still trains on every other token, so it learns when and how to look up, not the value itself.
The store holds 54.6M (entity, relation, value) triples extracted from Wikipedia by an 8B annotator trained on GPT-4o-labeled seeds.
At inference, a lookup token pauses generation; the query is matched by embedding similarity (rejection threshold 0.6) and the value is inserted.
Budget: about 3B tokens, 8 epochs, at most 8 H100-days per model.
FOLLOW-UP: CO-LMLM (JULY 2026)
Replaces relational triples with vector keys and free-text values, so knowledge is no longer limited to entity attributes.
About 2.2 ms per retrieval versus 28.6 ms for a question-generating lookup, which fits inside a voice turn.
Reports that lookup timing is imperfect: forcing lookups added 5.9 points on SimpleQA.[15]

WHAT HAS TO BE TRUE FOR REGULATED CONTACT CENTERS
Extraction quality: turning CRM, EHR and policy text into rows relies on LLM annotators; their errors become confident wrong answers.
Beyond entity facts: contact center knowledge is often procedural ("if the plan is X and the claim is Y"). Free-text values help, unproven here.
Reliable lookups: the model must know when to look up and must not fall back on weights.
Live data still needs tools: balances and appointments change by the minute; LMLM covers static knowledge, not transactions.
Total cost versus RAG: pre-training a private small model plus building and refreshing the store must beat retrieval on a frontier API.
Evidence at scale: nothing yet on dialog, speech, models above about 400M, or enterprise data. A 2026 position paper argues for this direction but reports no deployments.[66]

T11 · TOOLING AND IMPLEMENTATION

What to borrow, what to avoid
FRONTEND AND DEVELOPER TOOLING

Vercel's AI Voice Elements give React components for persona, mic capture, transcript and playback; useful for web prototypes, irrelevant to telephony.[82] Developer sentiment on AI is split, 49% positive and 42% negative, with 62% reporting burnout at some point; plan change management for internal builders too.[83]

WHY IMPLEMENTATIONS FAIL

Abstract goals instead of concrete use cases, bots isolated from scheduling and CRM systems, no handoff path, messy knowledge, and no measurement after launch.[84] vendor OpenAI's own rebuild relied on a silent production test to find failures synthetic load tests missed.[50][85]
